\section{Structured local-code construction and finite certificate}
\label{sec:structured-local-code}

This section gives the second main construction line.  Unlike the random sliding dense construction, the outer and inner
building blocks are short local codes supplied through explicit spectra or certified spectrum envelopes.  The first two
subsections define the local-code-independent proof interface.  The third subsection records the current proved finite
instantiation, and the final subsection is only a projection tool for possible BCH-like upgrades.

\subsection{Local-code outer certificate interface}
\label{subsec:local-code-outer-interface}

The structured construction replaces the sliding random-banded outer by a direct sum of short local codes.  The proof
interface is local-code independent: the outer contributes a weight enumerator, the interleaver turns each outer word of
weight \(h\) into a uniform slice input, and the inner contributes an upper bound \(p_h^{\rm in}(\delta)\) for that slice.
Systematic form is not required for the outer code.

\begin{lemma}[Block-outer first-moment interface]
\label{lem:block-outer-first-moment-interface}
Let \(C_{\mathrm{loc}}\subseteq\F_2^{n_0}\) be a binary linear \([n_0,b]\) code with homogeneous weight enumerator
\[
W_{\mathrm{loc}}(z)=\sum_j A^{\mathrm{loc}}_j z^j,
\]
and let \(C_{\mathrm{out}}=C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\oplus M}\).  Its message length is \(k=Mb\), its outer/interleaver length is
\(N=Mn_0\), and its weight enumerator is
\[
W_{\mathrm{out}}(z)=W_{\mathrm{loc}}(z)^M .
\]
Write \(A_h^{\mathrm{out}}=[z^h]W_{\mathrm{out}}(z)\), omitting the all-zero word from the nonzero-codeword sum.  If
\(p_h^{\rm in}(\delta)\) is any valid upper bound on the chosen inner construction producing output weight at most
\(\lfloor\delta N\rfloor\) from an interleaved outer word of weight \(h\), then
\[
\Pr[d_{\min}(\Csc)\le \lfloor\delta N\rfloor]
\le
\mu_{\mathrm{block}}(\delta)
:=
\sum_{h=1}^{N} A_h^{\mathrm{out}}p_h^{\rm in}(\delta).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The direct-sum enumerator identity follows by multiplying the local generating functions block by block.  Conditional on
any nonzero outer word of weight \(h\), the uniform interleaver randomizes only the support positions seen by the inner
code, so the same inner bound \(p_h^{\rm in}(\delta)\) applies to every such word.  Summing these failure probabilities
over all nonzero outer words gives \(\mu_{\mathrm{block}}(\delta)\), and Markov's inequality gives the minimum-distance
bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finite block-certificate template]
\label{lem:finite-block-certificate-template}
In the setting of Lemma~\ref{lem:block-outer-first-moment-interface}, suppose the nonzero outer weights are partitioned
into a finite list of audited row families \(\mathcal R\).  For each row \(R\in\mathcal R\), suppose a verifier or proof
establishes
\[
\sum_{h\in R} A_h^{\mathrm{out}}p_h^{\rm in}(\delta)\le 2^{-\lambda_R}.
\]
If the rows cover every nonzero outer word and every inner placement/episode case used by the bound, then
\[
\Pr[d_{\min}(\Csc)\le\lfloor\delta N\rfloor]
\le
\sum_{R\in\mathcal R}2^{-\lambda_R}.
\]
In particular, proving the right-hand side is at most \(2^{-\lambda}\) gives failure probability at most \(2^{-\lambda}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is bookkeeping after Lemma~\ref{lem:block-outer-first-moment-interface}.  The row hypotheses form a union-bound
cover of the first-moment sum over all nonzero outer words and all inner cases, and summing the row bounds gives the
claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Full-split local-code recursive inner}
\label{subsec:fullsplit-local-code-inner}

The full-split inner is also local-code independent.  Let \(C_{\rm in}\subseteq\F_2^{2b}\) be a binary linear
\([2b,b,d_0]\) code with encoder \(E:\F_2^b\to C_{\rm in}\) and local weight enumerator
\[
W_{\rm in}(z)=\sum_{w=0}^{2b} A_w^{\rm in}z^w .
\]
Partition the interleaved outer word into blocks \(U_i\in\F_2^b\) and keep a state \(S_i\in\F_2^b\).  Starting from
\(S_0=0\), form
\[
V_i=U_i+S_{i-1}.
\]
If \(V_i=0\), emit \(Y_i=0\) and set \(S_i=0\).  If \(V_i\ne0\), choose a fresh scrambler \(A_i\in GL_b(2)\), encode
\(C_i=E(A_iV_i)\), choose a fresh random coordinate permutation \(\tau_i\) of the \(2b\) codeword coordinates, and split
\[
(Y_i,S_i)=\operatorname{split}_b(\tau_i C_i),
\]
where the first \(b\) coordinates are emitted and the last \(b\) coordinates become the next state.

\begin{lemma}[One-step full-split law]
\label{lem:fullsplit-one-step-law}
Conditional on \(V_i\ne0\), the codeword \(C_i\) is uniform over the nonzero words of \(C_{\rm in}\).  Therefore the joint
law of emitted weight \(Y=\wt(Y_i)\) and next-state weight \(Q=\wt(S_i)\) is
\[
\Pr[Y=y,Q=q]
=
\frac{A^{\rm in}_{y+q}}{2^b-1}
\frac{\binom{b}{y}\binom{b}{q}}{\binom{2b}{y+q}}.
\]
If the incoming state has support size \(q\) and the next input block has support size \(r\), then the pre-encoding turnoff
atom is
\[
\Pr[V_i=0\mid q,r]
=
\begin{cases}
\binom{b}{q}^{-1}, & q=r,\\
0, & q\ne r.
\end{cases}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For fixed nonzero \(V_i\), \(A_iV_i\) is uniform over \(\F_2^b\setminus\{0\}\), and \(E\) bijects this set with the
nonzero codewords of \(C_{\rm in}\).  Conditional on a codeword of weight \(y+q\), the random coordinate split is
hypergeometric, giving the displayed split law.  The turnoff atom \(V_i=0\) occurs exactly when the input support equals
the state support; conditioned on the two support sizes this has probability \(\binom{b}{q}^{-1}\) if \(q=r\) and is
impossible otherwise.
\end{proof}

Thus a full-split certificate needs the local inner spectrum \(A_w^{\rm in}\), the induced split law above, and a finite
or analytic episode ledger for summing \(A_h^{\rm out}p_h^{\rm in}(\delta)\).  The current proved instance uses the exact
extended-BCH \([128,64,22]\) spectrum, so \(b=64\).

\subsection{Proved RM/EBCH finite instantiation}
\label{subsec:rm-ebch-finite-instantiation}

The current finite theorem uses a direct sum of \(4096\) copies of \(RM(4,9)\), a binary \([512,256,32]\) code, as the
outer code.  Hence
\[
k=4096\cdot256=2^{20},\qquad N=4096\cdot512=2^{21}.
\]
The inner is the full-split construction above with the exact EBCH \([128,64,22]\) local spectrum.  The target relative
distance is \(\delta=.09\), so
\[
d=\lfloor .09N\rfloor=188743.
\]
The authoritative verifier is
\[
\begin{gathered}
\texttt{python scripts/verify\_fullsplit\_finite\_ledger.py}\\
\texttt{--write-manifest-json scripts/fullsplit\_finite\_ledger\_manifest.json}.
\end{gathered}
\]
The verifier is part of the proof object, not a curve fit.  It checks the exact local spectra used in the outer and inner
models, the finite row-family bounds in the first-moment ledger, and the coverage bookkeeping that every nonzero outer
weight and every inner placement regime used by the certificate is included.  The detailed row development is preserved
in the full-split exploration note; the main paper keeps the audited interface and the resulting bound.

The finite certificate input data are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{component} & \text{certified object}\\
\hline
\text{outer spectrum} & RM(4,9)\ [512,256,32]\ \text{exact local spectrum}\\
\text{outer direct sum} & 4096\ \text{blocks},\ N=2^{21}\\
\text{inner split law} & EBCH\ [128,64,22]\ \text{exact local spectrum},\ b=64\\
\text{target} & \delta=.09,\ d=188743\\
\text{manifest} & \texttt{scripts/fullsplit\_finite\_ledger\_manifest.json}.
\end{array}
\]

\begin{lemma}[Exact RM prefix support split certificate]
\label{lem:rm-prefix-support-split}
For the direct-sum \(RM(4,9)\) outer, the exact local spectrum gives no nonzero outer weights below \(32\).  The verifier
uses exact direct-sum RM coefficients for the prefix \(32\le h\le500\) and records the dominant supported term at
\(h=32\), first-active regime \(r=1\), gap bucket \(1\le\mathrm{gap}\le4000\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The local \(RM(4,9)\) minimum distance is \(32\), so every nonzero direct-sum outer word has weight at least \(32\).  The
coefficient extraction for \(W_{RM}(z)^{4096}\) through \(h=500\) is performed by the verifier and stored in the finite
ledger manifest.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Checked small-prefix ledger]
\label{cor:fullsplit-small-prefix-all-first-active}
At \(N=2^{21}\) and \(\delta=.09\), the checked prefix contribution for the exact RM outer and full-split EBCH inner is
\[
\log_2\mu_{32\le h\le500}^{\rm fullsplit}\le -37.278528.
\]
This prefix cover includes all first-active placements for all supported weights \(32\le h\le500\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Checked post-prefix ledger rows]
\label{cor:fullsplit-postprefix-checked-ledger}
The checked \(h\ge501\) row families included in the finite ledger have union-bound total
\[
\log_2\mu_{\rm checked,\ h\ge501}^{\rm fullsplit}
\le -182.259739.
\]
The dominant post-prefix row is the \(501\le h\le2000\) all-episode wrapper.  The remaining audited families cover
ultra-late placements, early endpoint rows, accelerated endpoint rows, paired accelerated rows, fixed-pole intervals,
and the complement-high range.  The full row table is preserved in the full-split exploration note and in the audit
packet.  Overlaps between row families are retained only as harmless union-bound overcount.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The manifest checks that these row families cover the post-prefix regimes used by the certificate: ultra-late placements,
late-window all-episode/fixed-pole intervals, early endpoint and paired endpoint intervals through \(N/2\), and the
complement-high region.  The displayed total is the log-sum of the audited row contributions, whose maximum is the
\(-182.259739\)-bit all-episode wrapper row.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Current finite first-moment checkpoint]
\label{cor:fullsplit-current-finite-checkpoint}
Let \(\mu_{\rm checked}(.09)\) denote the first-moment contribution of the checked row families above.  Then
\[
\log_2\mu_{\rm checked}(.09)
\le -37.278528.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Combine Corollaries~\ref{cor:fullsplit-small-prefix-all-first-active} and~\ref{cor:fullsplit-postprefix-checked-ledger}:
\[
\log_2\left(2^{-37.278528}+2^{-182.259739}\right)
\le -37.278528.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Checked finite RM/full-split first-moment certificate]
\label{thm:fullsplit-rm-finite-certificate-009}
Consider the finite checkpoint \(N=2^{21}\), \(b=64\), and \(\delta=.09\), so that
\[
d=\lfloor .09N\rfloor=188743 .
\]
Use the direct-sum \(RM(4,9)\) outer and the full-split EBCH inner above.  Let \(Z_d\) be the number of nonzero
concatenated codewords whose final weight is at most \(d\), with expectation taken over the random interleaver and the
random choices of the inner construction.  The checked finite ledger gives
\[
\mathbb E Z_d\le 2^{-37.278528}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\Pr[d_{\min}\le d]\le 2^{-37.278528}.
\]
In particular, with positive probability a sampled code has \(d_{\min}\ge188744\), and hence relative distance
\[
\frac{d_{\min}}{N}\ge \frac{188744}{2^{21}}>.09 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:block-outer-first-moment-interface} reduces the failure probability to the first moment
\(\sum_hA_h^{\rm out}p_h^{\rm in}(.09)\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:rm-prefix-support-split} removes all positive weights below
\(32\).  Corollary~\ref{cor:fullsplit-small-prefix-all-first-active} covers \(32\le h\le500\), while
Corollary~\ref{cor:fullsplit-postprefix-checked-ledger} covers \(h\ge501\).  Therefore every nonzero outer word is
included in the row families defining \(\mu_{\rm checked}(.09)\), and
Corollary~\ref{cor:fullsplit-current-finite-checkpoint} gives the displayed expectation bound.  Markov's inequality
applied to the nonnegative integer random variable \(Z_d\) gives the probability statement.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Spectrum-model outer projections}
\label{subsec:fullsplit-outer-spectrum-projections}

Everything above this line is part of the checked RM/EBCH certificate.  The material below is not a theorem upgrade; it
is a spectrum-model design comparison.

The proved checkpoint above uses the exact RM \([512,256,32]\) local spectrum.  As a finite-\(N\) design diagnostic, we
also compared two possible stronger outer floors while leaving the full-split EBCH inner ledger unchanged.  This is not a
theorem upgrade: the BCH rows use a spectrum model, not a certified local spectrum or a rigorous low-weight envelope.
The comparison script is
\[
\texttt{python scripts/compare\_outer\_modes\_fullsplit.py --delta 0.09}.
\]
It writes the reports
\[
\begin{gathered}
\texttt{scripts/outer\_mode\_comparison\_delta009.csv},\\
\texttt{scripts/outer\_mode\_comparison\_delta009.md}.
\end{gathered}
\]
At the locked checkpoint \(N=2^{21}\), \(\delta=.09\), and \(d=188743\), the modeled rows suggest that replacing the
RM outer by a BCH-like \([512,256,\ge62]\) local code could substantially improve the margin, while a \([256,128,38]\)
model is roughly comparable to the proved RM row and sensitive to low-weight-spectrum inflation.  This conclusion is
only a projection until an exact spectrum or proved low-weight envelope is supplied.

The detailed comparison table, inflation stress tests, and BCH spectrum notes are preserved in the outer-spectrum
comparison and BCH-spectra exploration notes.
