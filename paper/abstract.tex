\begin{abstract}
Binary codes used in correlation generation and proof systems need strong
distance, fast ordinary and transposed encoding, and regular memory access.
We introduce Single-Permutation INterleaved (SPIN) codes, which compose
a blockwise outer encoder, one global interleaver, and a recursive inner
encoder.

Our main result is a Structured SPIN family with rate $1/2$ and $O(N)$
bit operations for both encoding directions at its native output lengths
$N$. With probability $1-o(1)$ over setup, its minimum distance exceeds
$0.11N$. A first-moment analysis groups outer words by block occupancy
and weight, then bounds their contributions to low-weight codewords
after structured interleaving and recursive encoding.

For finite lengths, a BCH-derived outer constituent and a fixed recursive
inner give rate $1/2$ and relative distance greater than $0.10$, with setup
failure below $2^{-40}$. We certify this bound at message lengths
$2^{16}$, $2^{18}$, $2^{20}$, $2^{22}$, and $2^{24}$ using rigorous spectrum inequalities,
without assuming the constituent's exact weight spectrum.

For pseudorandom correlation generators (PCGs), SPIN's transposed encoder
computes $2^{20}$ $128$-bit correlation blocks in about $10$\,ms on one
Ryzen core. This is approximately $3\times$ faster than the original
rate-$1/2$ BAA codes of Kolesnikov et al.\ (CRYPTO
2026)~\cite{KolesnikovEtAl2026BA}. Ordinary encoding has similar cost.
Including roughly $8$\,ms for GGM tree expansion, we estimate an end-to-end
cost of about $20$\,ms per batch, or $50$ million oblivious transfers per second.

We apply SPIN in a Brakedown-style polynomial commitment scheme and
integrate that scheme into Flock. With conditional 100-bit PCS parameters,
commitment and one opening of $512$\,MiB take $509$\,ms on one core.
Within an optimized Flock implementation, replacing Ligerito with
SPIN--Brakedown improves prover throughput by $1.36$--$1.92\times$ at the
measured workloads, with larger proofs. The comparison gives both
backends the applicable shared implementation optimizations.\footnote{This work was created with the assistance of ChatGPT 5.6, 6.0.}
\end{abstract}
