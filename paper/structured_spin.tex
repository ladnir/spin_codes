\section{Structured SPIN}
\label{sec:structured-spin}

\subsection{From random mixing to structured mixing}

Random SPIN is a useful starting point. Its random outer blocks have a simple
expected spectrum. Its uniform interleaver erases coordinate information, and
its random recursive inner repeatedly introduces fresh mixing. These
properties make the first-moment proof relatively clean.

They also make the encoder expensive. The random recursive inner performs
fresh random mixing at every coordinate. Under the parameter schedule used in
Section~\ref{sec:random-spin}, its direct implementation costs more than
linear time. Fast encoders usually obtain their speed from repeated circuits,
fixed local maps, and predictable data movement. In this sense, fast codes are
usually highly structured.

Structured SPIN tries to retain the randomness required by the distance proof
while placing structure wherever the encoder needs speed. A useful way to
understand this balance is to follow a sparse message through the
construction. Sparse messages are not always the only difficult regime:
medium-weight messages can also matter because there are many of them.
Nevertheless, a message supported in one or two outer blocks is a useful
model of the construction's most fragile behavior. Such a message begins with
little support, so every later stage must prevent that support from remaining
localized or disappearing.

Suppose first that the message activates one outer block. Random SPIN would
encode this block with a random injection. To obtain a faster and more
explicit construction, we replace that injection with a constituent code
whose low-weight spectrum can be certified. The minimum-weight codewords are
important, but the proof must also control the nearby low-weight shells, which
may contain many more words. The required outer interface is therefore a
certified envelope for the relevant low-weight spectrum, not merely a
minimum-distance bound.

Weight alone does not yet describe a structured block output. Two words of
equal weight may occupy different coordinates, and those coordinate patterns
may behave differently in later stages. Structured SPIN therefore samples an
independent output permutation for every outer block. Conditioned on the
output weight, the permuted support is uniform within that block. Independence
across blocks also prevents a message from deliberately aligning a repeated
coordinate pattern across several active blocks. After this local
randomization, the first-moment argument can again describe a block output by
its weight.

The next question is where this support enters the recursive inner. A uniform
global interleaver can, with small probability, send most of an active block
near the end of the inner computation. The state then has too few remaining
steps in which to spread the support. One can reduce this risk by forcing each
outer block to have large output weight, but larger or denser outer blocks
increase the encoding cost.

Structured SPIN instead builds the desired spreading property into the
interleaver. Its global permutation is factored into simple stages. The
within-block permutation first removes the constituent code's coordinate
structure. A deterministic transpose then distributes the coordinates of
each block among regions spanning the inner computation. Finally, independent
shuffles within those regions randomize the precise locations. The transpose
is the structured part that rules out a deliberately late start in the
declared routing pattern. The region shuffles stop the message from
controlling the remaining alignment and supply the probability law needed by
the first-moment calculation.

At this point, we have replaced the random outer and the uniform global
interleaver. The remaining expensive component is the random recursive inner.
A direct random inner refreshes its mixing at every coordinate. Structured
SPIN instead amortizes that refresh over a group of coordinates. During the
cheap part of an inner step, fixed structured maps mix one input group with a
small persistent state and emit a group of output bits. These maps are fast
because they operate on whole groups and reuse a compact circuit. At the
refresh stage, multiplication by a sampled field element supplies the
uniform-state law used by the proof for each fixed nonzero state. The analysis
combines this refresh with certified information about the kernels and images
of the fixed maps.

The resulting encoder is neither fully random nor fully deterministic. Its
constituent codes and batched inner operations provide speed. Its independent
block permutations, region shuffles, and state refreshes supply randomness at
the interfaces where the distance proof uses it. The rest of this section
turns this design story into a parameterized construction.

\subsection{Construction at a glance}

We first define one direct member of the family. Choose a constituent length
$b$, a constituent dimension $k\le b$, a number $L$ of outer-block
positions, an inner step width $t$, and a state dimension $s$, with $t\mid L$.
Set
\begin{equation}
  N:=Lb,
  \qquad
  K:=Lk,
  \qquad
  e:=L/t,
  \qquad
  R:=N/t=be.
  \label{eq:structured-parameters}
\end{equation}
Thus the interleaver will have $b$ regions, each region will contain $L$
coordinates, and the inner will process each region in $e$ steps.

The encoder has the following dataflow:
\begin{equation}
\F_2^{Lk}
  \xrightarrow{\ O\ }
(\F_2^b)^L
  \xrightarrow{\ \Pi_{\mathrm{blk}}\ }
(\F_2^b)^L
  \xrightarrow{\ T\ }
(\F_2^L)^b
  \xrightarrow{\ \Pi_{\mathrm{reg}}\ }
(\F_2^L)^b
  \xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{serialize}\ }
\F_2^N
  \xrightarrow{\ I\ }
\F_2^N.
\label{eq:structured-dataflow}
\end{equation}
The next three subsections define these arrows. The block-diagonal outer $O$
controls the possible weights produced by each active message block. The
within-block permutations $\Pi_{\mathrm{blk}}$ erase the coordinate patterns
of those outputs. The transpose $T$ spreads every outer block across the $b$
regions, and the region permutations $\Pi_{\mathrm{reg}}$ randomize the
locations within each region. The recursive inner $I$ then processes the
serialized regions in $t$-bit steps. In short, the outer supplies a certified
weight profile, the route supplies a controlled support law, and the inner
turns that routed support into output weight.

There are two kinds of construction data. The fixed data are the integers
$(L,b,k,t,s)$, two linear maps
\[
  A:\F_2^s\to\F_2^t,
  \qquad
  C:\F_2^t\to\F_2^s,
  \qquad
  C\circ A=0,
\]
a basis identifying $\F_2^s$ with $\F_{2^s}$, and a joint setup law for
injective constituent maps
\[
  G_j:\F_2^k\to\F_2^b
  \qquad (j\in[L]).
\]
This joint law must state whether the positions use one fixed constituent, one
shared sampled constituent, or independently sampled constituents. The
distinction changes the expected outer enumerator.

Setup samples the constituent maps from this joint law. Independently, it
samples one uniform coordinate permutation for every outer block, one uniform
permutation for every region, and one uniform nonzero field multiplier for
every inner step. All routing permutations and inner multipliers are mutually
independent. After setup, every arrow in
\eqref{eq:structured-dataflow} is fixed. A family selector $\theta_m$ is a
sequence of these parameter choices and setup laws; we suppress $m$ until the
scalable instantiation in Section~\ref{sec:structured-scalable}.

\subsection{Block-diagonal outer}

Write a message as $u=(u_1,\ldots,u_L)$ with
$u_j\in\F_2^k$. The outer encoder is the direct sum
\begin{equation}
  O(u)
  :=
  \bigl(G_1(u_1),\ldots,G_L(u_L)\bigr)
  \in(\F_2^b)^L.
  \label{eq:structured-outer}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, a generator matrix for $O$ places the local generator matrices
on the diagonal. The most basic instantiation repeats one constituent
$G:\F_2^k\to\F_2^b$ in every position. The scalable construction below builds
this shared map from a parameterized base constituent and two accumulator
layers. The current certificate uses an extended Golay base constituent, but
the construction interface does not fix that choice or its length.

The outer proof interface is its local weight enumerator. For a realized local
map $G_j$, define
\begin{equation}
  P_{G_j}(z)
  :=
  \sum_{v\in\F_2^k\setminus\{0\}}z^{\wt(G_j(v))}.
  \label{eq:local-constituent-enumerator}
\end{equation}
If one deterministic map $G$ is repeated, the number of messages with exactly
$q$ active blocks and outer weight $h$ is
\begin{equation}
  \binom Lq [z^h]P_G(z)^q.
  \label{eq:repeated-constituent-enumerator}
\end{equation}
For independently sampled, identically distributed local maps, expectation
replaces $P_G$ in \eqref{eq:repeated-constituent-enumerator} by
$\E[P_{G_1}]$. If one sampled map is reused in all positions, the correct
quantity is instead
\begin{equation}
  \binom Lq\E\bigl[[z^h]P_G(z)^q\bigr].
  \label{eq:shared-constituent-enumerator}
\end{equation}
In particular, an expectation over one sampled constituent must not be moved
inside the $q$th power.

The distance proof need not know how the local encoder is implemented. It may
consume an exact enumerator, an expected enumerator under the declared setup,
or a coefficientwise certified envelope. Minimum distance alone is generally
insufficient because nearby low-weight shells can have much larger
multiplicity.

\subsection{Structured interleaver}

Index the outer coordinates by pairs $(j,r)\in[L]\times[b]$, where $j$ is
the outer-block position. For every $j\in[L]$, setup samples
$\sigma_j\gets S_b$ and defines
\[
  \Pi_{\mathrm{blk}}(j,r):=(j,\sigma_j(r)).
\]
The deterministic transpose changes the coordinate view by
\[
  T(j,r):=(r,j)\in[b]\times[L].
\]
For every region $r\in[b]$, setup samples $\pi_r\gets S_L$ and defines
\[
  \Pi_{\mathrm{reg}}(r,j):=(r,\pi_r(j)).
\]
The complete interleaver is
\begin{equation}
  \Pi
  :=
  \Pi_{\mathrm{reg}}\circ T\circ\Pi_{\mathrm{blk}}.
  \label{eq:structured-interleaver}
\end{equation}
We serialize the output of \eqref{eq:structured-interleaver} in region-major
order. Thus region $r$ occupies the consecutive coordinates
$(r-1)L+1,\ldots,rL$.

Each outer block contributes exactly one coordinate to each of the $b$
regions. Conditional on the local output weight, $\sigma_j$ makes its active
coordinates a uniform subset of the regions. Conditional on every region
weight, $\pi_r$ makes the support uniform within that region. These two
symmetries remove the original coordinate pattern while preserving the
deterministic spreading supplied by the transpose.

\subsection{Structured recursive inner}

The inner processes the serialized interleaver output in $t$-bit steps. Write
that output as
\[
  X=(X_0,\ldots,X_{R-1}),
  \qquad
  X_i\in\F_2^t,
  \qquad
  R:=N/t=be.
\]
The state space $\F_2^s$ uses the fixed basis of $\F_{2^s}$. Fix linear maps
\[
  A:\F_2^s\to\F_2^t,
  \qquad
  C:\F_2^t\to\F_2^s,
  \qquad
  C\circ A=0.
\]
Setup samples $\alpha_i\gets\F_{2^s}^{\times}$ independently for
$0\le i<R$. Starting from $Q_0:=0$, the inner computes
\begin{equation}
  Y_i:=X_i+A(Q_i),
  \qquad
  Q_{i+1}:=\alpha_iQ_i+C(X_i)
  \quad(0\le i<R).
  \label{eq:structured-inner}
\end{equation}
It outputs $I(X):=(Y_0,\ldots,Y_{R-1})$ and discards $Q_R$. Field
multiplication refreshes a nonzero state once per $t$ output coordinates.
The fixed maps $A$ and $C$ perform the cheaper work inside each step. The
identity $C\circ A=0$ supports the transfer analysis and the optimized
transpose; it is not needed for injectivity.

\begin{lemma}[Inner bijectivity]
\label{lem:structured-inner-bijective}
For every fixed multiplier schedule and every fixed pair of linear maps
$A,C$, the map $I:(\F_2^t)^R\to(\F_2^t)^R$ defined in
\eqref{eq:structured-inner} is bijective.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $Y_0,\ldots,Y_{R-1}$. The initial state is known. At step $i$, recover
\[
  X_i=Y_i+A(Q_i),
\]
and then compute $Q_{i+1}$ from \eqref{eq:structured-inner}. This procedure
recovers exactly one input sequence from every output sequence.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Composition and rate}

\begin{definition}[Structured SPIN]
\label{def:structured-spin}
For a selector $\theta_m$ and one realization of its setup, the Structured
SPIN encoder is
\begin{equation}
  E_m
  :=
  I_m\circ\Pi_m\circ O_m
  :\F_2^{K_m}\longrightarrow\F_2^{N_m}.
  \label{eq:structured-spin-encoder}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Every realized local map is injective. The outer direct sum is therefore
injective. The interleaver is a permutation, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:structured-inner-bijective} makes the inner bijective. Hence
every realized Structured SPIN encoder is injective and has rate
\begin{equation}
  \frac{K_m}{N_m}=\frac{k_m}{b_m}.
  \label{eq:structured-spin-rate}
\end{equation}
The distance analysis can now vary the constituent family without changing
the routing or inner definitions. It must supply a certified outer spectrum
or envelope and a conditional failure envelope for the structured route
classes used in Theorem~\ref{thm:class-first-moment}.

The scalable family below lets the base constituent and $b_m$ vary while
retaining the fixed RM2Sub step and state dimensions. Finite-length
instantiations may tune these choices at one target length. Both cases use
Definition~\ref{def:structured-spin}.

\subsection{Scalable constituent family}
\label{sec:structured-scalable}

The scalable outer begins with an even base length $a$ and an injective
rate-one-half map
\[
  H_a:\F_2^{a/2}\longrightarrow\F_2^a.
\]
Its weight enumerator
\[
  W_a(z):=\sum_{v\in\F_2^{a/2}}z^{\wt(H_a(v))}
\]
must be known exactly or bounded by a certified envelope. For any multiple
$b$ of $a$, let $H_{a,b}$ be the direct sum of $b/a$ copies of $H_a$.
Setup samples two independent permutations $\tau_1,\tau_2\gets S_b$ and
constructs the rate-one-half BA-3 constituent
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal O_{a,b}
  :=
  \Acc_b\circ\tau_2\circ
  \Acc_b\circ\tau_1\circ H_{a,b}.
  \label{eq:ba3-outer}
\end{equation}
It samples $\mathcal O_{a,b}$ once and reuses that realization in every outer
position. This is the chosen-block form of the BA construction
\cite{KolesnikovEtAl2026BA,PecenyRindal2026ChosenBlockBAA}.

The base length $a$ is a construction parameter. A distance certificate must
bind a particular $H_a$, its spectrum, and an admissible schedule for $b$.
Changing $a$ or $H_a$ changes the outer enumerator and may change the certified
schedule. It does not change the route or the structured inner.

\paragraph{Current certified choice.}
For the theorem below, set $a:=24$ and let
\[
  H_{24}:\F_2^{12}\longrightarrow\F_2^{24}
\]
encode the extended binary Golay code. Fix a positive integer $m$ and set
$L_m:=128m$. Let $b_m$ be the least positive multiple of $24$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
  b_m\ge \frac{39}{4}\log_2(L_mb_m),
  \qquad
  N_m:=L_mb_m,
  \qquad
  K_m:=N_m/2.
  \label{eq:structured-native-schedule}
\end{equation}
Then $b_m=\frac{39}{4}\log_2N_m+O(1)$, and consecutive direct lengths have relative
gap $o(1)$.

Use $\mathcal O_{24,b_m}$ from \eqref{eq:ba3-outer}. Setup reuses this sampled
map in all $L_m$ diagonal positions:
\[
  G_{m,1}=\cdots=G_{m,L_m}:=\mathcal O_{24,b_m}.
\]
The expectation over $(\tau_1,\tau_2)$ therefore remains outside the
$q$th enumerator power, as in
\eqref{eq:shared-constituent-enumerator}.

After sampling this shared constituent, setup follows the generic
construction. It samples one independent $\sigma_j\gets S_{b_m}$ for every
outer position and one independent $\pi_r\gets S_{L_m}$ for every region.
The inner fixes $(t,s)=(128,19)$ and the RM2Sub-S19 maps $A,C$. It samples one
independent $\alpha_i\gets\F_{2^{19}}^\times$ per inner step. All route
permutations and multipliers are independent of $(\tau_1,\tau_2)$.

\begin{theorem}[Scalable Structured SPIN]
\label{thm:structured-spin-scalable}
For the setup above,
\begin{equation}
  \Prob\left[
    d_{\min}(E_m)\le\lfloor0.11N_m\rfloor
  \right]
  =o(1)
  \qquad(m\to\infty).
  \label{eq:structured-spin-distance}
\end{equation}
The probability includes the two BA permutations, all route permutations,
and all RM2Sub multipliers. Every realized code has rate $1/2$. Its ordinary
and transposed encoders use $O(N_m)$ bit operations.
\end{theorem}

Appendix~\ref{app:structured-scalable-certificate} records the certified
outer event, the occupation split, and the outward numerical inequalities
used in Theorem~\ref{thm:structured-spin-scalable}. The block constant $39/4$
is the current safe certificate value. Tightening it changes
\eqref{eq:structured-native-schedule}, but not the code architecture.

For any sufficiently large requested output length $n$, let $m(n)$ be the
largest index satisfying $N_{m(n)}\le n$. Define
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde E_n(x)
  :=
  E_{m(n)}(x)\Vert 0^{,n-N_{m(n)}}.
  \label{eq:structured-requested-length-wrapper}
\end{equation}
This zero extension preserves injectivity and minimum distance. Consecutive
direct lengths have relative gap $o(1)$, so
$N_{m(n)}/n=1-o(1)$. The wrapped family therefore has rate
$1/2-o(1)$, relative distance greater than $0.11-o(1)$ with high probability,
and $O(n)$ ordinary and transposed work. The native-length theorem remains
the primary statement.

\subsection{Finite-length specializations}

At a fixed target length, Definition~\ref{def:structured-spin} permits
hand-tuned outer and routing parameters. In particular, the base length $a$
and base constituent $H_a$ need not match the current Golay certificate. The
current $N=2^{21}$ evaluation target retains RM2Sub-S19, but its outer
constituent remains under selection. Appendix~\ref{app:structured-finite-member}
records the open certification and evaluation boundary for this
specialization.
