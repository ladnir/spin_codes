\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Binary codes inside correlation generators and proof systems are algorithms,
not merely stored generator matrices. They must combine constant rate and
linear distance with fast ordinary and transposed encoding and regular data
movement.

Pseudorandom correlation generators apply a public generator matrix to large
vectors to expand sparse correlations into OT and VOLE correlations
\cite{bcgi18,bcg19pcg}. Code-based proof systems encode rows of large arrays,
with distance supporting soundness~\cite{brakedown23,blaze25}. Transposition is
also operational: for a linear encoder $E$ and check vector $q$, the identity
$\langle E(x),q\rangle=\langle x,E^\top(q)\rangle$ pulls the check back to the
message coordinates. At these dimensions, long-range memory traffic can cost
more than the binary arithmetic.

Random linear codes provide excellent distance, but their encoders are dense.
Fast structured encoders reduce the computation and make memory access more
regular.
Their sparse dependencies, however, make linear distance difficult to prove.
The central question is whether one binary code family can achieve strong
distance, linear-time evaluation in both directions, and predictable data
movement.

This paper answers that question with Structured SPIN. At its native lengths
$N$, every realized code has rate $1/2$ and $O(N)$ ordinary and transposed
encoding work. With probability $1-o(1)$ over setup, its minimum distance
exceeds $0.11N$. One global factored interleaver contains the long-range data
movement; the outer permutations remain inside logarithmic-size blocks. A
  zero-extension wrapper gives rate $1/2-o(1)$, relative distance $0.11-o(1)$,
  and linear work at every sufficiently large requested length.

The construction follows the serial-concatenation pattern. An outer encoder
prevents messages from remaining too sparse. A recursive inner encoder spreads
the interleaved outer word across the final codeword. The interleaver moves the
outer support before the recursion processes it. SPIN makes this pattern
modular: structure appears where speed and regular data movement require it,
and setup randomness remains at the interfaces used by the distance proof.

For an admissible output length $N$, a SPIN encoder has the form
\begin{equation}
  E_N := I_N\circ\Pi_N\circ O_N.
  \label{eq:intro-spin-composition}
\end{equation}
The outer encoder $O_N$ maps the message into $N$ intermediate bits.
The interleaver $\Pi_N$ is one permutation of those $N$ coordinates.
The inner encoder $I_N$ is a length-preserving recursive linear map.
Setup may sample any of these objects from a declared distribution.

The phrase \emph{single permutation} describes the mathematical interface.
An implementation may factor $\Pi_N$ into block shuffles, a transpose, and
region shuffles. These factors still compose to one global interleaver.

\subsection{A family of SPIN families}

We use the same interface for three construction lines.

\paragraph{Accumulator SPIN.}
The outer encoder uses random block maps along the diagonal.
The interleaver is uniform, and the inner encoder is the binary accumulator.
This is the depth-two, one-accumulator truncation of the Block-Accumulate
architecture of Kolesnikov et al.~\cite{KolesnikovEtAl2026BA}. The BA
construction also supplies the random-block enumerator used below.
Chosen-block BAA instead studies the related option of reusing one certified
constituent in every outer position
\cite{PecenyRindal2026ChosenBlockBAA}.
The accumulator admits an exact input--output enumerator, making this construction
the simplest setting in which to expose the distance mechanism.

\paragraph{Random SPIN.}
The outer encoder and uniform interleaver remain unchanged. A random recursive
convolution replaces the accumulator. The stronger inner mixing yields a
substantially larger relative distance, at the cost of a denser recursion.
Silver codes earlier used a banded lower-triangular solve, which is
a structured recursive encoder of convolutional form
\cite{CouteauRindalRaghuraman2021Silver}. Expand-Convolute later isolated a
random time-varying convolution after a sparse expander
\cite{RaghuramanRindalTanguy2023EC}. The theorem below combines that random
inner with independent random-block injections.

\paragraph{Structured SPIN.}
A spectrum-controlled local outer, a structured global interleaver, and a
  structured recursive inner replace the random components. The scalable
  family samples one spectrum-certified BA-3 constituent and reuses it in every
  outer position. Its base code and base length are parameters. Independent
  block and region shuffles surround a deterministic transpose, and RM2Sub-S19
  supplies the inner recursion. The direct family has natural admissible
  lengths. Zero extension from the largest preceding direct member gives a
  code at every sufficiently large requested length. A separately tuned
  implementation at $N=2^{21}$ is a finite specialization, not the family
  definition.

The component table also defines a larger design space. For example, one may
combine a structured interleaver with a random outer and an accumulator.
This paper does not analyze every combination. An additional combination
enters the main development only when it supplies a necessary proof bridge or
a distinct quantitative result.

\subsection{Contributions}

We make four contributions.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A structured linear-time code with proved distance.}
  We construct a sampled binary code family whose native members have rate
  $1/2$ and minimum distance greater than $0.11N$ with probability $1-o(1)$.
  Every realized member has $O(N)$ ordinary and transposed encoding work. The
  result therefore combines strong binary distance with linear work in both
  evaluation directions.

  \item \textbf{A modular structured construction.}
  Structured SPIN combines one sampled BA-3 constituent reused along the outer
  diagonal, one factored global interleaver, and the fixed RM2Sub-S19 inner.
  The base constituent and its length are parameters exposed through a
  certified spectrum envelope. The interleaver contains the long-range data
  movement; the remaining outer permutations are block-local. Constituent
  replacement therefore does not change the route or inner architecture.

  \item \textbf{A first-moment framework for structured routes.}
  We index outer words by classes and bound each class's conditional failure
  probability under the interleaver--inner pair. Equal-weight words need not
  have the same inner law. The Structured SPIN proof uses occupation and
  row-weight classes.

  \item \textbf{Two explanatory SPIN families.}
  At rate $1/2$, Accumulator SPIN proves relative distance $0.0037$, while
  Random SPIN proves relative distance $0.11002$. These baselines isolate the
  block spectrum, uniform interleaving, and recursive mixing.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Related work}

\paragraph{Serially concatenated codes.}
Turbo codes introduced randomized interleaving as a way to combine simple
constituent encoders~\cite{berrou93}. Benedetto et al. developed the
uniform-interleaver analysis of serial concatenations~\cite{benedetto98}.
Divsalar, Jin, and McEliece introduced repeat--accumulate codes and their
weight-enumerator analysis~\cite{divsalar98}, while Kahale and Urbanke studied
minimum distance in random parallel and serial concatenations
~\cite{kahale98}. Bazzi, Mahdian, and Spielman proved limitations for
depth-two constant-memory serial concatenations and positive results with two
accumulators~\cite{bms09}. Later work established coding theorems for
repeat-multiple-accumulate ensembles approaching the binary
Gilbert--Varshamov bound~\cite{kliewer08}.

Block-Accumulate codes replace repetition with random block-diagonal maps and
compute detailed expected spectra; they also provide optimized CPU and GPU
implementations~\cite{KolesnikovEtAl2026BA}. Accumulator SPIN is their
one-accumulator truncation. Chosen-block BAA studies the complementary choice
of reusing one constituent in every diagonal position
~\cite{PecenyRindal2026ChosenBlockBAA}. Structured SPIN adopts that shared-code
view, but adds a structured global route and a different recursive inner.

\paragraph{Codes for correlation generation.}
Linear codes compress sparse correlations in vector-OLE and pseudorandom
correlation generators~\cite{bcgi18,bcg19pcg}. This application motivated a
sequence of faster structured codes. Silver uses structured LDPC matrices and
a banded triangular solve~\cite{CouteauRindalRaghuraman2021Silver}. Linear
distance was an explicit conjecture supported by heuristic evidence.
Expand--Accumulate combines a sparse expander with an accumulator
~\cite{ea22}. Expand--Convolute replaces that accumulator with a random
time-varying convolution~\cite{RaghuramanRindalTanguy2023EC}. Random SPIN keeps
the latter inner distribution but replaces the expander with random block
injections. Structured SPIN instead combines a spectrum-controlled BA outer,
one factored global interleaver, and the RM2Sub inner. Its principal distinction
is the simultaneous proof of rate one half, relative distance $0.11$, and
linear ordinary and transposed encoding work.

\paragraph{Linear-time proving systems.}
Constant-overhead arguments make linear-time encoding a first-order prover
cost~\cite{rr25}. Orion uses code switching to obtain a linear-time prover
~\cite{orion22}, and Brakedown builds a field-agnostic SNARK from a
linear-time encodable code~\cite{brakedown23}. Field-agnostic SNARKs have also
been instantiated from Expand--Accumulate codes~\cite{fieldEA24}. Blaze uses
interleaved RAA codes and supplies improved binary distance bounds
~\cite{blaze25}. SPIN does not instantiate a proof system in this paper. These
works motivate the combination of high rate, strong binary distance, fast
transposed evaluation, and regular data movement.

\subsection{One distance calculation}

All three lines use the same first-moment idea. Fix a target distance $d$,
and count nonzero messages whose encoded words have weight at most $d$.
If the expected count is below one, then some sampled encoder is injective and
has minimum distance greater than $d$.

For a uniform interleaver, the outer Hamming weight is often a sufficient
interface. The expected bad-word count then has the form
\begin{equation}
  \sum_w A_w^{\mathrm{out}}p_w^{\mathrm{in}}(d),
  \label{eq:intro-weight-first-moment}
\end{equation}
where $A_w^{\mathrm{out}}$ is the expected outer spectrum and
$p_w^{\mathrm{in}}(d)$ is an inner slice-to-tail probability.

A structured interleaver need not produce a uniform Hamming slice. We
therefore allow a class to record block occupation or another routing profile.
The interleaver--inner analysis supplies a uniform failure envelope for each
class. The weight-indexed sum in~\eqref{eq:intro-weight-first-moment} is the
special case in which the conditional law depends only on weight.
