\section{Finite lengths, asymptotics, and complexity}
\label{sec:scaling}

Finite validity, asymptotic distance, and encoding complexity are separate
properties. We state them separately for each SPIN family.

\subsection{Native and requested lengths}

The direct constructions apply at native lengths for which their block
dimensions are integral. Their logarithmic schedules permit admissible lengths
with relative gaps \(o(1)\). For Structured SPIN, the zero extension in
\eqref{eq:structured-requested-length-wrapper} maps the largest native member
below any sufficiently large requested length into that output length. This wrapper preserves
injectivity and absolute minimum distance. It changes rate and relative
distance by only $o(1)$ and preserves linear work.

\subsection{Asymptotic statements}

Theorems~\ref{thm:independent-accumulator-spin}
and~\ref{thm:independent-random-spin} give positive relative distance with
high probability for the independent-block ensembles. They specify the block
schedule, setup failure probability, and rate. At rate one half, the stated
examples use block constants \(3\) and \(17\), respectively; Random SPIN also
uses memory constant \(51/50\).

Theorem~\ref{thm:structured-spin-scalable} uses direct lengths
$N_m=L_mb_m$, where $L_m=128m$ and $b_m$ is the least multiple of $24$
satisfying $b_m\ge\frac{39}{4}\log_2N_m$. These lengths have relative gaps $o(1)$.
The requested-length wrapper gives rate $1/2-o(1)$, relative distance
$0.11-o(1)$, and linear work.

\subsection{Finite parameter extrapolation}
\label{sec:finite-parameter-extrapolation}

The asymptotic schedules do not identify the fastest finite member.  We use
finite calculations to order candidates before running a complete
certificate.  The present comparison targets relative distance $0.10$; a
larger distance is treated as additional slack.

Fix a rate-one-half outer constituent
$C:\F_2^{B/2}\rightarrow\F_2^B$.  The outer encoder repeats this same map in
every row.  For message length $k$, output length $N=2k$, and
$d=\lfloor0.10N\rfloor$, let
\begin{equation}
  \widehat U(C,k,t,s;d)
  :=
  \sum_{\tau\in\mathcal T}
  \widehat A^{\mathrm{out}}_\tau
  \widehat Q_\tau(d)
  \label{eq:finite-parameter-first-moment}
\end{equation}
be the computed class-indexed first-moment envelope from
\eqref{eq:finite-certificate}.  Define its margin by
\begin{equation}
  \widehat\Lambda(C,k,t,s;d)
  :=-\log_2\widehat U(C,k,t,s;d).
  \label{eq:finite-parameter-margin}
\end{equation}
The setup samples the row permutations, region permutations, and independent
nonzero RM2Sub multipliers.  The constituent $C$ remains fixed.  If a
construction samples $C$, the final failure bound must also include the
probability that its certified spectrum event fails.

Equation~\eqref{eq:finite-parameter-margin} has two distinct uses.  Outward
arithmetic and complete occupation coverage make it a certified failure
margin.  Binary64 arithmetic or incomplete occupation coverage makes it only
a diagnostic margin.  An estimated outer spectrum produces an estimated
margin.  A random outer spectrum is a reference ensemble and does not describe
the repeated fixed constituent.

The current RM2Sub landscape gives the following occupation-one diagnostics.
All rows use exact BCH spectra, $t=64$, distance $0.10$, and the matched
persistence rule $s+\log_2t=\log_2k+2$.

constituent & $B$ & $d(C)$ & margin at $k=2^{20}$ \\
\midrule
extended BCH $[32,16,8]$ & 32 & 8 & 2.846 \\
shortened XBCH $[64,32,12]$ & 64 & 12 & 10.089 \\

These values measure one active outer row.  They do not certify the complete

For experiment planning, we fitted the 15 BCH rows with
$B\in\{32,64,128\}$ and $16\le e:=\log_2k\le20$ to
\begin{equation}
  \widehat\Lambda_1
  =-14.754+2.142d(C)-0.471(e-20).
  \label{eq:bch-diagnostic-fit}
\end{equation}
The root-mean-square residual is $0.343$ bits, and the largest absolute
residual is $0.862$ bits.  Leaving out one message exponent at a time raises
the root-mean-square error to $0.417$ bits.  Leaving out one block size at a
time raises it to $1.137$ bits, with maximum error $1.941$ bits.  These checks
support interpolation within the measured slice.  The fit is not a spectrum
theorem and should not receive a confidence level.

The observed distances in the BCH-derived rate-one-half ladder at lengths
$32,64,128,256$ are respectively $8,12,22,38$.  The last point is the
deterministic $[256,128,\ge38]$ subcode, not a pure BCH constituent.  The size
heuristic
\begin{equation}
  d_{\mathrm{BCH\text{-}derived}}(B)
  \approx1.188\frac{B}{\log_2B}
  \label{eq:bch-distance-size-heuristic}
\end{equation}
fits these four values.  Combining
\eqref{eq:bch-diagnostic-fit} and
\eqref{eq:bch-distance-size-heuristic} projects a 40-bit occupation-one
threshold near $B=157$ at $k=2^{20}$.  The next power-of-two candidate is
therefore $B=256$.  This projection orders the next experiment; it does not
assert that the BCH-derived $[256,128,\ge38]$ subcode has a 40-bit RM2Sub
certificate.  That assertion requires its certified spectrum input and every
occupation.

The inner schedule uses the persistence coordinate
\begin{equation}
  p:=s+\log_2t.
\end{equation}
The diagnostic curves remain roughly level when $p=\log_2k+2$.  The complete
RM$(4,9)$ certificate instead anchors the more conservative offset
$p=\log_2k+4$.  Write $t=2^m$.  The selected map $A$ is an $s$-dimensional
subcode of RM$(2,m)$, so it requires
\begin{equation}
  s\le1+m+\binom m2.
  \label{eq:rm2sub-capacity}
\end{equation}
At fixed persistence, the tested shorter epochs gave stronger margins.
Accordingly, the initial certified-track staircase takes the smallest
$m\ge6$ that satisfies~\eqref{eq:rm2sub-capacity}.  It gives:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
$\log_2k$ & 16 & 20 & 24 & 28 & 32 & 36 \\
\midrule
$t$ & 64 & 64 & 64 & 128 & 128 & 256 \\
$s$ & 14 & 18 & 22 & 25 & 29 & 32 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The table is an admissible search schedule, not a transfer theorem.  Each new
$(t,s)$ pair needs audited maps, exact map spectra, and a complete finite
calculation.  The offset-two staircase is the corresponding performance
candidate and should be tested in the same parameter scan.

Missing BCH spectra can enter through exact coefficients, certified shell
caps, or an explicitly labeled estimate.  For a fixed $w\le r$, one unbiased
estimator samples a uniform $r$-subset $S\subseteq[B]$ and enumerates the
shortened code
\[
  C_S:=\{c\in C:\supp(c)\subseteq S\}.
\]
If $X_w(S)$ counts its weight-$w$ words, then
\begin{equation}
  \E_S[X_w(S)]
  =A_w(C)\frac{\binom rw}{\binom Bw},
  \qquad
  \widehat A_w
  :=\overline X_w\frac{\binom Bw}{\binom rw}
  \label{eq:shortening-spectrum-estimator}
\end{equation}
is unbiased.  The expectation is over independent uniform $r$-subsets.
Before this estimate guides parameters, it must reproduce the exact BCH
$[128,64,22]$ SPIN functional on held-out samples.  It must also agree across
several shortening sizes and random seeds.  Without a concentration bound,
the resulting row remains estimated rather than certified.

The finite selector therefore proceeds in two stages.  It first uses
\eqref{eq:bch-diagnostic-fit}, the RM2Sub staircase, and spectrum estimates to
rank admissible tuples.  It then computes every occupation with outward
arithmetic and benchmarks only the surviving implementations.  For requested
margin $\lambda_{\mathrm{target}}$, the selector accepts a tuple only when the
complete setup bound is at most $2^{-\lambda_{\mathrm{target}}}$.  The
extrapolation never substitutes for this final test.

\subsection{Random-family encoding costs}

Under either proved schedule, the outer encoder processes \(N/B\) blocks of
length \(B=\Theta(\log N)\). Direct dense block multiplication therefore
costs \(\Theta(N\log N)\) bit operations. The accumulator costs \(O(N)\).
The Random SPIN recurrence evaluates \(m=\Theta(\log N)\) taps per output
coordinate and hence costs \(\Theta(N\log N)\). These bounds concern direct
encoders; preprocessing or word-level batching may reduce practical constants.

\subsection{Cost model}

Let $C_{\mathrm{out}}(b)$ be the work to encode one outer block, and let
$C_{\mathrm{in}}(t,s)$ be the work for one structured inner step. A
Structured SPIN encoder has the schematic cost
\begin{equation}
  T(N)
  =
  \frac{N}{b}C_{\mathrm{out}}(b)
  +
  \frac{N}{t}C_{\mathrm{in}}(t,s)
  +O(N),
  \label{eq:structured-cost}
\end{equation}
where the last term covers routing and permutation work.

For any fixed base constituent with a constant-size encoding circuit, tiling,
two permutations, and two accumulators give
$C_{\mathrm{out}}(b)=O(b)$. The fixed RM2Sub-S19 step has constant cost.
Every term in \eqref{eq:structured-cost} is therefore $O(N)$. The current
certificate uses extended Golay as the base constituent, but the cost argument
does not depend on its 24-bit length.
