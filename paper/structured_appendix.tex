\section{Asymptotic Structured SPIN certificate}

\subsection{Certificate for the scalable family}
\label{app:structured-scalable-certificate}

This subsection proves Theorem~\ref{thm:structured-spin-scalable}. The proof
first selects one Golay--BA-3 constituent whose realized spectrum obeys a
certified envelope. It then reduces the structured route to occupation and
mean row weight. The final first-moment bound separates four regimes:
$Q=1,2$, fixed $Q\ge3$, growing sparse $Q$, and positive relative
occupation. Finite RM2Sub transfers handle the last two regimes. Continuum
transfers handle the fixed regimes.

For $0\le d\le N$ and $1\le Q\le L$, let $Z_{d,Q}$ count nonzero messages
with exactly $Q$ active outer blocks and encoded weight at most $d$. Unless a
display states otherwise, expectations and probabilities include the shared
BA constituent, the route permutations, and the RM2Sub multipliers. The
finite-length specialization is not part of this probability space.

\subsubsection{The one-sampled Golay--BA-3 outer}

We begin with the exact expected spectrum before selecting the shared
constituent. Let
\[
  G(u):=1+759u^8+2576u^{12}+759u^{16}+u^{24}
\]
be the weight polynomial of the extended Golay code, and define
\[
  G_b(a):=[u^a]G(u)^{b/24}.
\]
For $a\ge1$, put $r_a:=\lceil a/2\rceil$ and
\begin{equation}
  T_b(a,c)
  :=
  \binom{c-1}{r_a-1}\binom{b-c}{a-r_a},
  \qquad
  P_b(a,c):=\frac{T_b(a,c)}{\binom ba}.
  \label{eq:structured-acc-transition}
\end{equation}
We set $T_b(0,0):=1$ and $T_b(0,c):=0$ for $c\ne0$. Binomial coefficients
outside their natural range are zero. Thus $P_b(a,c)$ is the exact
weight-transition probability of a uniformly interleaved accumulator.

For a realized BA constituent $\mathcal O_b$, let
$A_{\mathcal O_b}(w)$ count its nonzero words of weight $w$, and define
\[
  \overline A_b(w)
  :=
  \E_{\tau_1,\tau_2}[A_{\mathcal O_b}(w)].
\]

\begin{lemma}[Exact expected BA spectrum]
\label{lem:structured-exact-ba-spectrum}
For every $0\le w\le b$,
\begin{equation}
  \overline A_b(w)
  =
  \sum_{a=0}^b\sum_{c=0}^b
  G_b(a)P_b(a,c)P_b(c,w).
  \label{eq:structured-exact-ba-spectrum}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix a Golay direct-sum word of weight $a$. The first uniform permutation and
accumulator produce weight $c$ with probability $P_b(a,c)$. Conditioned on
that weight, the second permutation makes the word uniform on its Hamming
slice. The second accumulator then produces weight $w$ with probability
$P_b(c,w)$. Summing over the $G_b(a)$ input words proves
\eqref{eq:structured-exact-ba-spectrum}.
\end{proof}

The exact formula supplies both the tail bound and the spectrum envelope.
Let
\[
  \mathcal W:=[0.104,0.896].
\]
The outer certificate proves
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{w/b\notin\mathcal W}\overline A_b(w)=o(1).
  \label{eq:structured-ba-tail}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[One-sample selection]
\label{lem:structured-one-sample-selection}
There is an event $\mathcal G_b$, determined by the two BA permutations, such
that
\begin{equation}
  \Prob[\mathcal G_b]=1-o(1).
  \label{eq:ba-good-event}
\end{equation}
On $\mathcal G_b$, every nonzero constituent word has relative weight in
$\mathcal W$, and
\begin{equation}
  A_{\mathcal O_b}(w)
  \le b^2\overline A_b(w)
  \qquad(0\le w\le b).
  \label{eq:ba-selected-spectrum}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The expected number of nonzero tail words equals the left side of
\eqref{eq:structured-ba-tail}. Markov's inequality shows that no such word
exists with probability $1-o(1)$. For each remaining weight $w$, another
application of Markov's inequality gives
\[
  \Prob[A_{\mathcal O_b}(w)>b^2\overline A_b(w)]\le b^{-2}.
\]
A union bound over at most $b+1$ weights costs $O(1/b)$. Intersecting the two
events proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

The event in Lemma~\ref{lem:structured-one-sample-selection} is paid once.
After setup samples $\mathcal O_b$, the construction reuses that realization
at every outer position.

We next state the asymptotic envelope verified from
\eqref{eq:structured-exact-ba-spectrum}. Let
\[
  h(x):=-x\ln x-(1-x)\ln(1-x)
\]
with its continuous endpoint values. Define
\[
  g(a)
  :=
  \inf_{u>0}\left\{\frac1{24}\ln G(u)-a\ln u\right\}.
\]
On the accumulator feasibility region $a/2\le c\le1-a/2$, set
\[
  \pi(a,c)
  :=
  c h\!\left(\frac{a}{2c}\right)
  +(1-c)h\!\left(\frac{a}{2(1-c)}\right)-h(a),
\]
and set $\pi(a,c):=-\infty$ outside that region. The limiting exponent is
\begin{equation}
  a_{\rm BA}(x)
  :=
  \sup_{0\le a,c\le1}
  \{g(a)+\pi(a,c)+\pi(c,x)\}.
  \label{eq:structured-ba-variational}
\end{equation}
Uniform coefficient and Stirling bounds in
\eqref{eq:structured-exact-ba-spectrum} give
\begin{equation}
  \frac1b\ln\overline A_b(w)
  \le
  a_{\rm BA}(w/b)
  +O\!\left(\frac{\ln b}{b}\right).
  \label{eq:structured-ba-asymptotic}
\end{equation}

The certificate represents a concave majorant $\widehat a_{\rm BA}$ as the
lower envelope of rational affine supports. It proves
\begin{equation}
  a_{\rm BA}(x)\le\widehat a_{\rm BA}(x)
  \qquad(x\in\mathcal W).
  \label{eq:ba-spectrum-majorant}
\end{equation}
There are $31$ affine segments. The central support is the exact dimension
bound $\widehat a_{\rm BA}(x)\le(\ln2)/2$. The interval verifier processes
$101{,}910$ rational boxes. Its largest accepted outward upper endpoint is
\begin{equation}
  -2.2000492744786192\mathbin{\cdot}10^{-9}.
  \label{eq:structured-ba-majorant-margin}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:structured-ba-asymptotic} and
\eqref{eq:ba-spectrum-majorant} give the outer exponent used below. The
artifact retains all affine supports and box witnesses.

\subsubsection{What the structured route preserves}

View an outer word as an $L$-tuple of rows in $\F_2^b$. For the framework in
Section~\ref{sec:framework}, take its class to be the ordered row-weight
profile
\[
  c(u):=(\wt(u_1),\ldots,\wt(u_L)).
\]
This finite class records the information preserved by the independent block
shuffles. The analysis below constructs a uniform route--inner failure
envelope for every such profile. It then aggregates profiles by occupation and
mean row weight where those coarser parameters suffice.

Fix $Q$ active outer positions. Let their realized output weights be
$w_1,\ldots,w_Q$, and set
\[
  x_j:=\frac{w_j}{b},
  \qquad
  \alpha:=\frac QL,
  \qquad
  x:=\frac1Q\sum_{j=1}^Qx_j,
  \qquad
  q:=\alpha x.
\]
The next lemma justifies the reduction from the complete weight tuple to
$(\alpha,x)$ when $\alpha$ has a positive lower bound.

\begin{lemma}[Route domination]
\label{lem:structured-route-domination}
Let $F$ be any nonnegative function of the routed $L$-by-$b$ binary array.
Conditioned on the row weights $w_1,\ldots,w_Q$, the structured route
satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \E_{\rm route}[F]
  \le
  (b+1)^Q(L+1)^b
  \E_{\operatorname{Ber}(q)^{Lb}}[F].
  \label{eq:structured-route-domination}
\end{equation}
If $\alpha$ is bounded below by a positive constant, the multiplicative
factor in \eqref{eq:structured-route-domination} is $\exp(o(N))$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For row $j$, begin with $b$ independent Bernoulli-$x_j$ variables and
condition their sum to equal $w_j$. The value $w_j$ is a mode of the
binomial distribution. Hence the inverse conditioning probability is at
most $b+1$. Applying this reduction to all active rows costs at most
$(b+1)^Q$.

Before the region shuffle, one fixed region contains independent Bernoulli
variables whose average success probability is $q$. Let $S$ be their sum.
For every $0\le k\le L$, the Chernoff bound for a Poisson-binomial sum gives
\[
  \Prob[S=k]
  \le
  \exp\{-L D_{\rm KL}(k/L\Vert q)\}.
\]
The method-of-types lower bound gives
\[
  \Prob[\operatorname{Bin}(L,q)=k]
  \ge
  \frac1{L+1}
  \exp\{-L D_{\rm KL}(k/L\Vert q)\}.
\]
After a uniform region shuffle, both laws are uniform on every weight-$k$
slice. The routed region law is therefore pointwise at most $L+1$ times the
iid Bernoulli-$q$ law. Multiplying over the $b$ independent region shuffles
proves \eqref{eq:structured-route-domination}.

Finally,
\[
  \ln((b+1)^Q(L+1)^b)
  =Q\ln(b+1)+b\ln(L+1)=o(Lb)
\]
when $Q/L$ is bounded below by a positive constant and $b,L\to\infty$.
\end{proof}

Concavity of $\widehat a_{\rm BA}$ now reduces the selected outer spectrum
to the mean row weight:
\begin{equation}
  \frac1Q\sum_{j=1}^Q\widehat a_{\rm BA}(x_j)
  \le
  \widehat a_{\rm BA}(x).
  \label{eq:structured-ba-jensen}
\end{equation}
The region shuffles are essential for Lemma~\ref{lem:structured-route-domination}.
The deterministic transpose alone spreads each row, but it does not erase
the row's coordinate pattern.

\subsubsection{A finite RM2Sub transfer}

We next define the transfer matrix used after the route reduction. Identify
the RM2Sub state field with $\F_2^{19}$, and set
\[
  n:=2^{19},
  \qquad M:=n-1,
  \qquad \kappa:=\frac{M}{M-1}.
\]
The fixed state-output map $A$ has image weights
\[
  (d_i)_{i=0}^6=(0,48,56,64,72,80,128)
\]
with multiplicities
\[
  (c_i)_{i=0}^6=(1,5040,110848,292510,110848,5040,1).
\]
For $\xi,\eta\in\F_2^{19}$, define the integer association matrix
\begin{equation}
  \mathsf K_{ij}
  :=
  \sum_{\substack{\xi:\,\wt(A(\xi))=d_i}}
  \sum_{\substack{\eta:\,\wt(A(\eta))=d_j}}
  (-1)^{\langle \xi,\eta\rangle}.
  \label{eq:structured-association-matrix}
\end{equation}
The frozen circuit and an integer Walsh transform determine
$\mathsf K$ exactly.

Fix an iid input-bit probability $\beta\in(0,1)$ and a weight fugacity
$z\in(0,1)$. Put
\[
  u:=1-\beta+\beta z,
  \quad
  v:=\beta+(1-\beta)z,
  \quad
  r:=1-\beta-\beta z,
\]
and define vectors $\mathbf D,\mathbf F\in\mathbb R^7$ by
\[
  D_i:=u^{128-d_i}v^{d_i},
  \qquad
  F_i:=u^{128-d_i}r^{d_i}.
\]
Set
\begin{align}
  k_{00}&:=\frac1n\sum_i c_iF_i,
  &k_{01}&:=u^{128}-k_{00},
  \label{eq:structured-k00-k01}\\
  \overline\mu
  &:=\frac{n^{-1}\mathbf F^{\mathsf T}\mathsf K\mathbf D-k_{00}D_0}
  {k_{01}},
  &\mu&:=\frac{\sum_i c_iD_i-D_0}{M}.
  \label{eq:structured-mu-def}
\end{align}
The three state classes are zero ($Z$), freshly produced nonzero ($D$), and
uniform or punctured-uniform nonzero ($R$). Define
\begin{equation}
  T_3(\beta,z)
  :=
  \begin{pmatrix}
    k_{00}&k_{01}&0\\
    \overline\mu/M&0&\overline\mu\\
    \kappa\mu/M&0&\kappa\mu
  \end{pmatrix}.
  \label{eq:structured-t3}
\end{equation}
Let $e_Z:=(1,0,0)^{\mathsf T}$, and let $\1_3$ be the all-ones vector in
$\mathbb R^3$.

\begin{lemma}[Three-state RM2Sub envelope]
\label{lem:structured-t3-envelope}
For iid Bernoulli-$\beta$ inner input and zero initial state,
\begin{equation}
  \E\bigl[z^{\wt(Y)}\bigr]
  \le
  e_Z^{\mathsf T}T_3(\beta,z)^{N/128}\1_3.
  \label{eq:structured-t3-moment}
\end{equation}
The expectation is over the iid Bernoulli input bits and the independent
nonzero field multiplier in every inner step.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first row of \eqref{eq:structured-t3} is exact. Equation
\eqref{eq:structured-association-matrix} evaluates the output moment after a
transition from zero to a nonzero state, which gives $\overline\mu$. For a
uniform nonzero entering state, the image spectrum gives $\mu$.

For any fixed nonzero entering state, the fresh multiplier makes the
multiplied state uniform on $\F_{2^{19}}^\times$. Adding a nonzero syndrome
terminates the state with probability $1/M$. Conditioned on survival, the
next-state law omits at most one nonzero value. Replacing that law by a
uniform nonzero law costs at most $\kappa=M/(M-1)$. The last row pays this
factor. All entries are nonnegative, so transfer multiplication proves
\eqref{eq:structured-t3-moment}.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Positive occupation}

Fix $\alpha\ge10^{-4}$ and $x\in\mathcal W$. We compare the routed
Bernoulli-$q$ input, where $q=\alpha x$, with a reference bit probability
$\beta=py$. The binary relative-entropy chain rule gives
\begin{equation}
  D_{\rm KL}(\alpha x\Vert py)
  \le
  D_{\rm KL}(\alpha\Vert p)
  +\alpha D_{\rm KL}(x\Vert y).
  \label{eq:structured-kl-chain}
\end{equation}
For a nonnegative matrix $T$, let $\rho_{\mathrm{sp}}(T)$ denote its spectral
radius. A Collatz witness for $T$ is a positive vector $\boldsymbol\omega$
and a scalar $\lambda$ satisfying
$T\boldsymbol\omega\le\lambda\boldsymbol\omega$ entrywise; it certifies
$\rho_{\mathrm{sp}}(T)\le\lambda$. For $p,y,z\in(0,1)$, define
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \Phi(\alpha,x;p,y,z)
  :={}&
  \alpha\widehat a_{\rm BA}(x)
  +D_{\rm KL}(\alpha\Vert p)
  +\alpha D_{\rm KL}(x\Vert y)\\
  &+\frac1{128}\ln\rho_{\mathrm{sp}}(T_3(py,z))
  -0.11\ln z.
\end{split}
\label{eq:structured-positive-exponent}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Positive-occupation certificate]
\label{prop:structured-positive-certificate}
For every
$(\alpha,x)\in[10^{-4},1]\times\mathcal W$, there exist rational
$p,y,z\in(0,1)$ and a rational positive Collatz vector such that the outward
upper bound on \eqref{eq:structured-positive-exponent} is negative. The
largest accepted upper endpoint is
\begin{equation}
  -3.194048334767875\mathbin{\cdot}10^{-7}.
  \label{eq:structured-positive-margin}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Condition on $\mathcal G_b$ and a fixed set of $Q=\alpha L$ active rows.
Equations~\eqref{eq:ba-selected-spectrum} and
\eqref{eq:ba-spectrum-majorant} bound each row-weight multiplicity. Lemma
\ref{lem:structured-route-domination} replaces the structured route by iid
Bernoulli-$\alpha x$ input at subexponential cost. Equation
\eqref{eq:structured-ba-jensen} combines unequal row weights.

Changing the reference bit probability to $py$ costs the exponent in
\eqref{eq:structured-kl-chain}. Lemma~\ref{lem:structured-t3-envelope} gives
the RM2Sub moment. The Chernoff inequality contributes $-0.11\ln z$ per
output coordinate. These terms give \eqref{eq:structured-positive-exponent}.

The verifier partitions the $(\alpha,x)$ rectangle into $621$ rational
boxes. It fixes one witness $(p,y,z)$ and one Collatz vector before checking
the vertices of each box. For one affine support of
$\widehat a_{\rm BA}$, the exponent is convex in
$(\alpha,q=\alpha x)$. Four vertex checks therefore cover the box. All
logarithms and matrix operations use outward interval arithmetic. The
largest endpoint is \eqref{eq:structured-positive-margin}.
\end{proof}

The quantifier order in Proposition~\ref{prop:structured-positive-certificate}
is
\[
  \sup_{\alpha,x}\inf_{p,y,z}.
\]
The witnesses are proof parameters. They are not setup randomness.

\subsubsection{Fixed occupation}

The subexponential estimate in Lemma~\ref{lem:structured-route-domination}
is not uniform when $Q$ is fixed. We instead retain one coefficient
fugacity for each active row.

Set
\[
  r_0:=\frac1M,
  \qquad
  p_0:=\frac{2^{18}}M,
  \qquad
  P_0:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\r_0&1-r_0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For $\theta>0$, let $z_L:=e^{-\theta/L}$ and $\gamma:=p_0\theta$. Define
\[
  D_\gamma(t):=\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{-\gamma t}).
\]
If one region contains $a$ impulses, its normalized locations converge to
the order statistics of $a$ uniform points. Their spacings are uniform on
the $a$-simplex. Define the limiting region matrix
\begin{equation}
  K_a(\theta)
  :=
  a!\!\int_{\substack{t_i\ge0\\\sum_{i=0}^at_i=1}}
  D_\gamma(t_0)P_0D_\gamma(t_1)\cdots
  P_0D_\gamma(t_a)\,d\boldsymbol t.
  \label{eq:structured-ka}
\end{equation}
For $a=0$, this means $K_0(\theta):=D_\gamma(1)$.

For fixed $Q$, collision and boundary events change every nonzero entry by a
relative factor $1+O_Q(M/L)$. Since $b=O(\log L)$, their total logarithmic
cost across all regions is $o_Q(b)$. Thus \eqref{eq:structured-ka} has the
same exponential rate as the exact full-state region transfer.

Introduce a row fugacity $u_j>0$ for each active row and define
\begin{equation}
  T_Q(\boldsymbol u,\theta)
  :=
  \sum_{\boldsymbol e\in\{0,1\}^Q}
  \left(\prod_{j=1}^Qu_j^{e_j}\right)
  K_{\sum_je_j}(\theta).
  \label{eq:structured-tq-fugacity}
\end{equation}
Coefficient extraction enforces the row weights. For a fixed weight tuple,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \E\bigl[z_L^{\wt(Y)}\mid w_1,\ldots,w_Q\bigr]
  \le{}&
  \left(\prod_{j=1}^Q\binom b{w_j}^{-1}u_j^{-w_j}\right)\\
  &\cdot e_0^{\mathsf T}T_Q(\boldsymbol u,\theta)^b\1_2
  \exp(o_Q(b)).
\end{split}
\label{eq:structured-fixed-coefficient}
\end{equation}
Here $e_0:=(1,0)^{\mathsf T}$ and $\1_2:=(1,1)^{\mathsf T}$. The expectation
is over the route permutations and RM2Sub multipliers, conditioned on the
displayed row weights and the selected constituent.

We use one product norm for every fixed $Q\ge3$. Fix
$0<\sigma<1$, $\tau>0$, and $v_1>0$. Let
\[
  H_u:=I+uP_0,
  \qquad
  \boldsymbol v:=(1,v_1)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
For a nonnegative $2$-by-$2$ matrix $B$, define
\[
  \lVert B\rVert_{\boldsymbol v}
  :=\max\left\{(B\boldsymbol v)_0,
  \frac{(B\boldsymbol v)_1}{v_1}\right\}.
\]
Also define
\begin{align}
  G_Q(\sigma,\tau)
  &:={}
  \sup_{0\le y\le1}
  \left\{-\tau y+\left(1+\frac1Q\right)
  \ln(1-y+y/\sigma)\right\},
  \label{eq:structured-gq}\\
  m_{\boldsymbol v}(u)
  &:={}
  \max\left\{1+u\sigma v_1,
  \frac{ur_0}{v_1}+\sigma(1+u(1-r_0))\right\}.
  \label{eq:structured-mv}
\end{align}
The beta-integral representation of \eqref{eq:structured-ka}, followed by
the weighted row norm induced by $\boldsymbol v$, gives
\begin{equation}
  \left\|T_Q(\boldsymbol u,Q\tau/p_0)\right\|_{\boldsymbol v}
  \le
  \exp\{QG_Q(\sigma,\tau)\}
  \prod_{j=1}^Qm_{\boldsymbol v}(u_j).
  \label{eq:structured-product-norm}
\end{equation}
The logarithm in \eqref{eq:structured-gq} is nonnegative. Hence
$G_Q(\sigma,\tau)\le G_3(\sigma,\tau)$ for every $Q\ge3$.

For $x\in\mathcal W$, define the coupled row exponent
\begin{equation}
  R(x;u)
  :=
  \widehat a_{\rm BA}(x)-h(x)-x\ln u
  +\ln m_{\boldsymbol v}(u).
  \label{eq:structured-row-exponent}
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:ba-selected-spectrum},
\eqref{eq:structured-fixed-coefficient}, and
\eqref{eq:structured-product-norm} yields
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \ln\E[Z_{\lfloor0.11N\rfloor,Q}\mid\mathcal G_b]
  \le{}&Q\ln L\\
  &+b\sum_{j=1}^Q
  \left\{R(x_j;u_j)+G_3(\sigma,\tau)
  +\frac{0.11\tau}{p_0}\right\}
  +o_Q(b).
\end{split}
\label{eq:structured-fixed-exponent}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Weight-coupled fixed occupation]
\label{prop:structured-fixed-certificate}
Fix any integer $Q\ge3$. Under
\eqref{eq:structured-native-schedule},
\begin{equation}
  \Prob[Z_{\lfloor0.11N\rfloor,Q}>0\mid\mathcal G_b]
  \le
  \exp\{-0.0008689\,Qb+o_Q(b)\}.
  \label{eq:structured-fixed-final}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The certificate fixes
\[
  \sigma=\frac{127}{250},
  \qquad
  \tau=\frac{133}{125},
  \qquad
  v_1=\frac3{1600}.
\]
For each of the $31$ affine segments of $\widehat a_{\rm BA}$, it chooses one
positive rational fugacity $u$. For fixed $u$, the map
$x\mapsto R(x;u)$ is convex on that segment. It is therefore enough to check
the two endpoints. The resulting $62$ outward checks prove
\begin{equation}
  R(x;u)+G_3(\sigma,\tau)+\frac{0.11\tau}{p_0}
  <-0.07196093457399053.
  \label{eq:structured-fixed-row-margin}
\end{equation}
Under $b=(39/4)\log_2N+O(1)$, choosing an active position costs at most
\[
  \frac{4\ln2}{39}b+o(b).
\]
The largest outward endpoint after adding this cost is
$-0.0008689160550218037$. Equation
\eqref{eq:structured-fixed-exponent} and Markov's inequality prove
\eqref{eq:structured-fixed-final}. The factor $b^2$ in
\eqref{eq:ba-selected-spectrum} contributes only $o_Q(b)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[One- and two-row occupations]
\label{prop:structured-one-two-certificate}
Under \eqref{eq:structured-native-schedule}, the bad-message probabilities
for $Q=1$ and $Q=2$ satisfy
\[
  \Prob[Z_{\lfloor0.11N\rfloor,Q}>0\mid\mathcal G_b]=o(1).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The exact continuum transfers for $Q=1,2$ give a structured-outer gap greater
than $0.2592968543039901$ bits per active row. After the support choice, the
schedule-decay exponent is greater than $1.5281443294639036$ per active row.
These margins leave a strictly negative logarithmic first-moment exponent
after all counting costs. The hash-bound receipt verifies the stated outward
endpoints.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Growing sparse occupation}

It remains to cover $Q\to\infty$ with $Q/L\le10^{-4}$. In this regime, a
four-state transfer avoids paying the punctured-uniform factor $\kappa$ at
every step. Its state classes are zero ($Z$), freshly produced nonzero
($D$), uniform nonzero ($U$), and punctured-uniform nonzero ($P$).

Let $p$ be a mark probability and set the input-bit probability to
$\beta=p/2$ in \eqref{eq:structured-k00-k01} and
\eqref{eq:structured-mu-def}. Define
\[
  a(p)
  :=
  1-\frac1n\sum_{i=0}^6c_i(1-p)^{d_i}.
\]
For the parameter range used below, the following matrix is an entrywise
envelope:
\begin{equation}
  T_4(p,z)
  :=
  \begin{pmatrix}
  k_{00}&k_{01}&0&0\\
  a/M&0&\overline\mu-z^{128}a&a(M-1)/M\\
  a/M&0&\mu-z^{128}a&a(M-1)/M\\
  \kappa a/M&0&\kappa(\mu-z^{128}a)&\kappa a(M-1)/M
  \end{pmatrix},
  \label{eq:structured-t4}
\end{equation}
where $a=a(p)$. The fresh multiplier makes termination independent of the
current output after the state and input are fixed. This gives the factors
$1/M$ and $(M-1)/M$ in \eqref{eq:structured-t4}.

Set
\[
  p:=\frac85\alpha,
  \qquad
  z:=1-\frac85\alpha,
  \qquad
  \boldsymbol w
  :=
  \left(1,2^{-10},2^{-10},\kappa2^{-10}\right)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Exact rational Bernstein coefficients prove
\begin{equation}
  T_4(p,z)\boldsymbol w
  \le
  (1-99\alpha)\boldsymbol w
  \qquad(0<\alpha\le10^{-4}).
  \label{eq:structured-t4-collatz}
\end{equation}
The induced random-outer exponent satisfies
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  &\frac{\alpha\ln2}{2}
  +D_{\rm KL}(\alpha\Vert p)
  +\frac1{128}\ln(1-99\alpha)
  -0.11\ln z\\
  &\hspace{35mm}\le
  -\frac{780897}{6665600}\alpha.
\end{split}
\label{eq:structured-random-sparse-decay}
\end{equation}
The certificate proves \eqref{eq:structured-t4-collatz} after clearing all
positive denominators. The three nontrivial row polynomials have degrees at
most $511$. After their zero factors are removed, every Bernstein
coefficient is strictly negative.

To transfer \eqref{eq:structured-random-sparse-decay} to the selected BA
outer, define its likelihood excess relative to a random rate-one-half row:
\begin{equation}
  \ell(x)
  :=
  \widehat a_{\rm BA}(x)-h(x)+\frac{\ln2}{2}.
  \label{eq:structured-likelihood-excess-def}
\end{equation}
On each affine segment of $\widehat a_{\rm BA}$, the function $\ell$ is
convex. Outward endpoint checks prove
\begin{equation}
  \sup_{x\in\mathcal W}\ell(x)<0.01281.
  \label{eq:ba-likelihood-excess}
\end{equation}
Thus each active selected-outer row costs at most
$\exp((0.01281+o(1))b)$ relative to the random-outer counting measure.

\begin{proposition}[Growing sparse occupation]
\label{prop:structured-growing-sparse}
Suppose $Q\to\infty$ and $Q/L\le10^{-4}$. Then
\begin{equation}
  \E[Z_{\lfloor0.11N\rfloor,Q}\mid\mathcal G_b]
  \le
  \exp\{-(0.0325484+o(1))Qb\}.
  \label{eq:structured-growing-sparse-final}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equations~\eqref{eq:structured-random-sparse-decay} and
\eqref{eq:ba-likelihood-excess} leave the natural decay coefficient
\[
  \kappa_{\rm BA}
  :=
  \frac{780897}{6665600}-0.01281
  =\frac{86938833}{833200000}
  >0.10434329
\]
per active row bit. Choosing the $Q$ active rows costs at most
$(4\ln2/39+o(1))Qb$. The exact bound $\ln2<7/10$ gives
\[
  \kappa_{\rm BA}-\frac{4\ln2}{39}>0.0325484.
\]
The $b^2$ selection factor and the local-limit remainders contribute
$o(Qb)$ because $Q\to\infty$. This proves
\eqref{eq:structured-growing-sparse-final}.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Completion and certificate boundary}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:structured-spin-scalable}]
Condition on $\mathcal G_b$, and abbreviate
$Z_Q:=Z_{\lfloor0.11N\rfloor,Q}$. Proposition
\ref{prop:structured-one-two-certificate} covers $Q=1,2$, and Proposition
\ref{prop:structured-fixed-certificate} covers every fixed $Q\ge3$.
Proposition
\ref{prop:structured-growing-sparse} covers $Q\to\infty$ with
$Q/L\le10^{-4}$. Proposition
\ref{prop:structured-positive-certificate} covers $Q/L\ge10^{-4}$.

These cases cover every sequence of occupations. The fixed bounds tend to
zero for each fixed $Q$. The growing sparse bound is geometric on the $Qb$
scale, even after summing over the possible values of $Q$. The positive-range
bound is uniform and exponential on the $N$ scale. Markov's inequality and a
union bound therefore give
\[
  \Prob\left[\sum_{Q=1}^L Z_Q>0\,\middle|\,\mathcal G_b\right]=o(1).
\]
This is \eqref{eq:structured-spin-distance} conditioned on $\mathcal G_b$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:ba-good-event} removes the conditioning.

Rate follows from \eqref{eq:structured-spin-rate}. Each outer encoder uses
constant-size Golay circuits, two stored permutations, and two
accumulators, so it costs $O(b)$ in either circuit direction. The route and
the fixed RM2Sub steps cost $O(N)$. Thus the ordinary and transposed encoders
both use $O(N)$ bit operations.
\end{proof}
