\section{A Modular Serial Concatenation Framework}
\label{sec:sc-framework}

We study depth-2 serially concatenated convolutional encoders
\[
\Csc \;:=\; \Ctwo \circ \Pi \circ \Cone \;\subseteq\; \{0,1\}^n,
\]
where \(\Pi\) is a uniform random interleaver (uniform random permutation) on \([n]\).
Equivalently, writing generator matrices \(\Gone\in\{0,1\}^{k\times n}\), \(\Pi\in\{0,1\}^{n\times n}\),
and \(\Gtwo\in\{0,1\}^{n\times n}\), a codeword can be computed as
\[
y \;=\; x\cdot \Gone \cdot \Pi \cdot \Gtwo \in \{0,1\}^n
\qquad\text{for a message }x\in\{0,1\}^k.
\]
(Here \(\Ctwo\) is length preserving, hence represented by an \(n\times n\)
matrix.  It need not be invertible on all of \(\F_2^n\); the first-moment event
below certifies injectivity on the chosen outer code together with distance.  In
later sections \(\Ctwo\) may be drawn from a random inner ensemble.)

We follow the standard convention: \(\Cone\) is the outer code and \(\Ctwo\) is the inner code.

\paragraph{Goal of this section.}
This section factors the minimum-distance analysis into:
(i) an exact first-moment reduction, and
(ii) reusable outer/inner interfaces that upper-bound the two quantities appearing in that reduction.
The exact theorem communicates only through the outer spectrum \(\enum_{\Cone,w}\) and the inner slice-to-tail probabilities
\(p_w(\delta)\).
For some constructions, a single coarse envelope suffices; for others, we will use sharper weight-dependent envelopes on
different weight ranges.
The point is that the \emph{core theorem stays simple}, while the later instantiations are free to plug in whatever
collection of bounds gives the best certified distance.

\subsection{Outer interface: distance and spectrum}
\label{sec:outer-interface}

Let \(\Cone=\Cone(n)\subseteq\{0,1\}^n\) be a (possibly random) linear code of rate \(R_1\).
For each weight \(w\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}\), define the (expected) outer weight spectrum
\[
\enum_{\Cone,w} \;:=\; \E\Bigl[\bigl|\{c\in\Cone:\ \wt(c)=w\}\bigr|\Bigr],
\]
where the expectation is over the randomness of \(\Cone\) (and is omitted if \(\Cone\) is deterministic).

The exact framework theorem below only uses the numbers \(\enum_{\Cone,w}\).
In later sections we may upper-bound them by different expressions on different weight ranges.
As a convenient coarse package, we record the following single-envelope interface.

\begin{assumption}[Outer interface]
\label{ass:outer-interface}
Fix an outer-code ensemble \(\Cone=\Cone(n)\).
There exist constants \(\alpha>0\), \(c\ge 0\), and \(\beta>1\), depending on the ensemble but not on \(n\),
such that for all sufficiently large \(n\):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{(O1) Logarithmic distance:} \(d_{\min}(\Cone)\ge \alpha\log n\) with probability \(1-o(1)\).
\item \textbf{(O2) Spectral growth:} \(\enum_{\Cone,w}\le n^c\,\beta^w\) for all \(w\in\{1,\dots,n\}\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}




\noindent
Later sections instantiate \(\Cone\) by two different families:
a log-memory dense terminated convolutional outer for the asymptotic theorem, and a direct sum of short local codes for
the finite certificate.
When we want tighter end-to-end constants, we will work directly with more refined envelopes for \(\enum_{\Cone,w}\)
rather than insist on one global \(n^c\beta^w\) bound.

\subsection{Inner interface: contraction under a random interleaver}
\label{sec:inner-interface}

The role of the random interleaver is to randomize locations so that, conditioned on an outer weight \(w\),
the input to the inner code is uniform over the Hamming slice of weight \(w\).

\begin{lemma}[Interleaver makes a fixed weight-\(w\) word uniform on the slice]
\label{lem:interleaver-uniform-slice}
Fix any \(c\in\{0,1\}^n\) with \(\wt(c)=w\). If \(\Pi\) is a uniform random permutation of \([n]\),
then \(\Pi(c)\) is uniform over \(\{u\in\{0,1\}^n:\ \wt(u)=w\}\).
\end{lemma}

\noindent
\emph{Proof.}
Every weight-\(w\) vector is obtained from \(c\) by exactly \(w!\,(n-w)!\) permutations, and \(\Pi\) is uniform on \(S_n\). \(\square\)

\paragraph{Slice-to-tail probability (the object we need from the inner).}
Let \(U\) be uniform over \(\{0,1\}^n\) conditioned on \(\wt(U)=w\).
Define, for \(\delta\in(0,1)\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pw-def}
p_w(\delta)
\;:=\;
\Pr\bigl[\wt(\Ctwo(U))\le \lfloor\delta n\rfloor\ \big|\ \wt(U)=w\bigr].
\end{equation}
If \(\Ctwo\) itself is drawn from a random inner ensemble
(e.g. the dense time-varying recursion of Section~\ref{sec:inner-dense}
or the full-split local-code inner of Section~\ref{sec:fullsplit-local-code-inner}),
then the probability above is taken over both the inner randomness and \(U\).
(Equivalently, one may interpret \(p_w(\delta)\) as the average slice-to-tail probability under the inner ensemble.)
For example, later sections analyze the accumulator inner (Section~\ref{sec:inner-accumulator}),
the dense time-varying recursive inner (Section~\ref{sec:inner-dense}),
and the structured full-split inner used in the finite certificate (Section~\ref{sec:fullsplit-local-code-inner}).

\begin{assumption}[Inner interface]
\label{ass:inner-interface}
Fix a target relative distance $\delta\in(0,1)$.
There exist constants $\rho=\rho(\delta)\in(0,1)$ and $a\ge 0$ such that for all sufficiently large $n$ and all
\[
w \ \ge\ d_{\min}(\Cone),
\]
if $U$ is uniform over $\{0,1\}^n$ conditioned on $\wt(U)=w$, then the inner encoder $\Ctwo$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:inner-interface-pw}
p_w(\delta)
\;:=\;
\PP\big[\wt(\Ctwo(U))\le \lfloor\delta n\rfloor \ \big|\ \wt(U)=w\big]
\;\le\;
n^{a}\,\rho^{\,w}.
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}


\noindent
This is the formal version of the ``interleaver gain'' / small-output contraction phenomenon:
a random interleaved weight-\(w\) input produces an output below \(\delta n\) with probability exponentially small in \(w\),
up to a polynomial prefactor.
Again, this is only a coarse package: the exact theorem below can equally well consume any sharper weight-dependent upper
envelope \(p_w(\delta)\le B_w(\delta)\).

\subsection{The SC framework theorem (first-moment reduction)}
\label{sec:framework-theorem}

\begin{theorem}[SC framework theorem]
\label{thm:sc-framework}
Fix \(\delta\in(0,1)\). Let \(\Cone\subseteq\{0,1\}^n\) be any fixed linear
outer code and let \(\Ctwo:\F_2^n\to\F_2^n\) be any possibly random linear
inner map independent of the interleaver \(\Pi\).
Let \(\Csc=\Ctwo\circ\Pi\circ\Cone\).

Define the random variable counting low-weight nonzero concatenated codewords:
\[
X_\delta
\;:=\;
\bigl|\{c\in\Cone\setminus\{0\}:\ \wt(\Ctwo(\Pi(c)))\le \lfloor\delta n\rfloor\}\bigr|.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:first-moment-master}
\Pr\Bigl[
 \Ctwo\circ\Pi\big|_{\Cone}\text{ is not injective}
 \ \text{or}\ d_{\min}(\Csc)\le \lfloor\delta n\rfloor
\Bigr]
\;\le\;
\EE[X_\delta]
\;=\;
\sum_{w=d_{\min}(\Cone)}^{n} \enum_{\Cone,w}\cdot p_w(\delta),
\end{equation}
where \(p_w(\delta)\) is defined in \eqref{eq:pw-def}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because the concatenated map is linear, \(X_\delta\ge1\) occurs exactly when
its restriction to \(\Cone\) has a nonzero kernel element or its image has a
nonzero word of weight at most \(\lfloor\delta n\rfloor\).  Thus by Markov's
inequality,
\[
\Pr\Bigl[
 \Ctwo\circ\Pi\big|_{\Cone}\text{ is not injective}
 \ \text{or}\ d_{\min}(\Csc)\le \lfloor\delta n\rfloor
\Bigr]
\;=\;
\Pr[X_\delta\ge 1]
\;\le\;
\EE[X_\delta].
\]
To compute \(\EE[X_\delta]\), group outer codewords by weight \(w\).
For each fixed \(c\in\Cone\) with \(\wt(c)=w\), Lemma~\ref{lem:interleaver-uniform-slice} implies that \(\Pi(c)\)
is uniform over the Hamming slice of weight \(w\), hence
\[
\Pr[\wt(\Ctwo(\Pi(c)))\le \lfloor\delta n\rfloor \mid \wt(c)=w]
\;=\; p_w(\delta),
\]
which depends only on \(w\). Summing over all codewords of each weight yields
\[
\EE[X_\delta]=\sum_w \enum_{\Cone,w}p_w(\delta),
\]
and \eqref{eq:first-moment-master} follows. \(\square\)
\end{proof}

\subsection{Reusable envelope corollary}
\label{sec:framework-corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Envelope corollary]
\label{cor:framework-envelope}
Fix \(\delta\in(0,1)\), and let \(w_0=w_0(n)\in\{1,\dots,n\}\).
Assume the outer-code ensemble, the interleaver, and the inner-code ensemble are mutually independent.
Suppose:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Outer cutoff:} \(\Pr[d_{\min}(\Cone)< w_0]\le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{out}}(n)\).
\item \textbf{Outer envelope:} for every \(w\in\{w_0,\dots,n\}\),
\[
\EE[\enum_{\Cone,w}] \;\le\; A_w.
\]
\item \textbf{Inner envelope:} for every \(w\in\{w_0,\dots,n\}\),
\[
p_w(\delta)\;\le\; B_w.
\]
\end{enumerate}
Then
\[
\Pr\Bigl[
 \Ctwo\circ\Pi\big|_{\Cone}\text{ is not injective}
 \ \text{or}\ d_{\min}(\Csc)\le \lfloor\delta n\rfloor
\Bigr]
\;\le\;
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{out}}(n)\;+\;\sum_{w=w_0}^{n} A_w\,B_w.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let \(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{out}}\) be the event \(d_{\min}(\Cone)\ge w_0\).
Let \(\mathcal B_\delta\) denote the joint bad event in the statement.  Split
according to \(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{out}}\):
\[
 \Pr[\mathcal B_\delta]
 \le \Pr[\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{out}}^{c}]
     +\Pr[\mathcal B_\delta\wedge\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{out}}].
\]
Condition on the realization of \(\Cone\) on the event \(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{out}}\).
Since \(d_{\min}(\Cone)\ge w_0\), Theorem~\ref{thm:sc-framework} gives
\[
\Pr[\mathcal B_\delta\mid\Cone,\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{out}}]
\;\le\;
\sum_{w=w_0}^{n}\enum_{\Cone,w}\,p_w(\delta).
\]
Take expectation over \(\Cone\) and use the assumed envelopes:
\[
\Pr[\mathcal B_\delta\wedge\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{out}}]
\;\le\;
\sum_{w=w_0}^{n}\EE[\enum_{\Cone,w}]\,B_w
\;\le\;
\sum_{w=w_0}^{n}A_w\,B_w.
\]
Adding \(\Pr[\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{out}}^{c}]\le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{out}}(n)\) proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{How the rest of the paper uses the framework.}
Later sections prove outer spectrum bounds and inner slice-to-tail bounds, then plug them into
Theorem~\ref{thm:sc-framework} or Corollary~\ref{cor:framework-envelope}.  The accumulator warmup and random sliding
dense theorem use analytic envelopes; the RM/EBCH finite construction uses a manifest-backed ledger for the same
first-moment sum.
