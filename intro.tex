\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
A central goal in coding theory and in cryptography-facing applications is to obtain linear minimum distance together with
streaming encodability: the encoder should process the input in one pass, produce output symbols incrementally, and use
small working memory together with near-linear work.  Classical turbo-style constructions suggest that random interleaving
and recursion can produce excellent distance behavior in practice, but converting this intuition into
clean, modular, proof-driven guarantees, especially under stringent work and locality constraints, remains subtle.

\paragraph{Our viewpoint: serial concatenation as a modular distance amplifier.}
We study depth-2 serial concatenation with a random interleaver:
\[
\Csc \;:=\; \Ctwo \circ \Pi \circ \Cone \subseteq \F_2^n,
\qquad
G_{\mathrm{sc}} \;=\; \Gone \cdot \Pi \cdot \Gtwo,
\]
where \(\Gone\) and \(\Gtwo\) are matrices for the outer map \(\Cone\) and
the length-preserving recursive inner map \(\Ctwo\), and \(\Pi\) is a uniform
random interleaver on \([n]\).  A square inner map need not be globally
invertible, so the framework controls the joint event that the concatenation
is noninjective on the outer code or has a low-weight nonzero image word.
Our main conceptual contribution is to separate the minimum-distance proof into two independently verifiable interfaces:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Outer interface:} (i) logarithmic minimum distance \(d_{\min}(\Cone)\ge \alpha\log n\), and
(ii) a spectrum bound \(\enum_{\Cone,w}\le \poly(n)\beta^w\) on the weight regime used by the first-moment argument.
\item \textbf{Inner interface:} a slice-to-tail bound for a random interleaved input of weight \(w\),
\[
p_w(\delta)\ :=\ \Pr\big[\wt(\Ctwo(U))\le \delta n \,\big|\, \wt(U)=w\big],
\]
where \(U\) is uniform over the Hamming slice \(\{0,1\}^n\) conditioned on \(\wt(U)=w\).
\end{itemize}

Once these interfaces are established, a short first-moment calculation yields linear minimum distance for \(\Csc\) with high probability
(over \(\Pi\) and any inner randomness).

\paragraph{Target performance characteristics.}
Our design goals are the following.
We want (i) provable linear minimum distance \(d_{\min}(\Csc)=\Omega(n)\) with high probability over the randomized construction
(interleaver and any internal randomness), (ii) streaming/online encoding with small working memory (ideally \(O(\log n)\) or \(O(1)\) state),
and (iii) near-linear work, measured by tap complexity: the number of state coordinates read/XORed per output symbol.
Concretely, we aim for \(O(1)\) taps per symbol (hence \(O(n)\) total taps) with a single random interleaver.
Our modular framework makes these tradeoffs explicit: the outer code controls the low-weight spectrum, the interleaver randomizes support,
and the inner recursion supplies contraction and large-deviation behavior.


\paragraph{Outer instantiations.}
We keep two outer viewpoints in the main paper.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Random sliding dense outer.}
A terminated, feedforward convolutional outer with logarithmic memory and dense taps within the band.  This is the
analytic baseline for the asymptotic dense+dense theorem.
\item \textbf{Structured local-code outer.}
A direct sum of short local codes, used through its exact or certified weight spectrum.  This is the current finite
certificate route.  The proved rows use either \(4096\) copies of \(RM(4,9)\)
or \(16384\) EBCH blocks restricted by one explicit XOR-parity block.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Inner instantiations.}
We likewise organize the inner side by what proof role it serves.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Accumulator inner.}
The simplest recursive rate-$1$ inner.  It admits an exact input--output enumerator via a spacing/compositions count.
This provides a clean warmup proof vehicle, but its certified constants are weak.
\item \textbf{Dense time-varying recursive inner (log memory).}
A log-memory streaming recursion with fresh dense random taps at each time step.
While the encoder is \(O(n\log n)\)-work, it yields a strong three-mechanism tail bound for \(p_w(\delta)\)
(early-first-one, rare ON\(\to\)OFF termination, and a large-deviation term),
and a forced-termination refinement that turns termination into a geometric-in-\(w\) contraction for small/intermediate weights.
\item \textbf{Full-split local-code recursive inner.}
A block recursion whose active block is encoded by a short rate-half local code and randomly split between emitted
coordinates and next state.  The current finite certificate uses the exact EBCH \([128,64,22]\) spectrum.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Putting the modules together.}
The framework first gives a warmup theorem: the random sliding dense outer paired with the accumulator inner already has
linear distance, although with weak constants.  The two main construction lines then improve this baseline.  The random
sliding dense outer paired with the dense recursive inner gives a clean asymptotic linear-distance theorem, including the
concrete \(0.109\) relative-distance checkpoint.  The structured local-code construction gives a finite certificate:
\(RM(4,9)\) direct-sum outer plus the full-split EBCH inner at \(N=2^{21}\), \(\delta=.09\), with rational/outward first moment
at most
\[
\frac{6793\cdot2^{130}+1}{2^{180}}\le 2^{-37.27}.
\]
Thus some realization is an actual binary
\([2^{21},2^{20},d_{\min}\ge188744]\) code, not merely a
length-preserving encoder with an unverified dimension.
Here the complete \(h\le500\) first-active subfamily contributes at most
\(6793/2^{50}\), while the complementary range \(h\ge501\) contributes at most
\(2^{-180}\).

For encoding vectors over \(\F_{2^{128}}\), an explicit second row replaces
the RM outer by \(16384\) EBCH \([128,64,22]\) blocks and makes the last
64-symbol message block the componentwise XOR of the preceding \(16383\).
Every nonzero message then activates at least two local blocks, giving outer
weight at least \(44\).  The exact certificate proves
\[
 \mathbb E Z_d\le\frac{417}{2^{50}}\le2^{-41.29},
\]
and hence the existence of a binary
\([2^{21},1048512,d_{\min}\ge188744]\) code.  Scalar extension of its binary
generator gives the same parameters in symbol Hamming distance over
\(\F_{2^{128}}\).  BCH-spectrum projections beyond these two exact rows remain
heuristic.
