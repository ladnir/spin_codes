# Canonical-octet count refinements

This note records local outer-count arguments for the current binary code.
It does not certify a new distance or a new whole-code failure probability.
The H5-plus-incidence helper remains unchanged. The intersection refinement
is implemented as a separate optional helper; H6 packing remains a candidate.

## Objects and current premises

Let (C\subseteq\mathbb F_2^{256}) be the production code generated by
`spin/src/kernels/generated/BchCircuit.h`. Its dimension is 128. Partition
the coordinates into 32 consecutive octets. For a set (T\subseteq[32]),
let (C_T) contain the words of (C) supported inside those octets, and
write (d_T=\dim C_T). A uniform shortening cap (d(u)) satisfies

\[
\dim(C\text{ shortened to }S)\le d(u)
\quad\text{for every coordinate set }S\text{ of size }u.
\]

The retained, authenticated dimension table supplies

\[
d(48)=d(56)=1,\qquad d(64)=2,\qquad d(160)=66.
\]

These are uniform coordinate-subset bounds. No canonical-specific result
below lowers that global table.

For (r\in\{1,2,3,4\}), let (R_r(d)) count ordered four-row tuples of
rank (r) in a binary space of dimension (d):

\[
R_r(d)=
\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(2^d-2^i)(16-2^i)}
     {\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(2^r-2^i)},
\]

with value zero when (r>d). Let (F_r(h)) count such tuples in (C^4)
whose joint canonical support contains at most (h) octets.

The production generator file has SHA256
`c13131ca9d02861ac5b4688849a4550196120abdce5c5aa0bec7df71eac02424`.

## Implemented: exact exclusion through five octets

`outer_hill_octets.check_production()` freshly checks the generator rank,
even parity, divisibility by the BCH polynomial, and its 36 consecutive
nonzero field roots. The BCH bound and even extension give distance at
least 38. The helper then exhausts all 201,376 five-octet sets.

A word in five octets has weight 38 or 40. It is therefore the full
40-position mask with zero or two positions removed. A quotient-map
syndrome table checks these possibilities exactly. The production scan
finds no nonzero word. Fewer than five octets are excluded by distance.
Consequently (F_r(h)=0) for every (h\le5) and every rank (r\ge1).

This is specific to the canonical octets. It does not prove that the code
has no weight-38 or weight-40 words elsewhere.

## Implemented: divide by incidence multiplicity

A tuple with exactly (s\le h) active octets lies in

\[
\binom{32-s}{t-s}\ge\binom{32-h}{t-h}
\]

sets of (t\ge h) octets. Summing tuples inside all (t)-octet sets gives

\[
F_r(h)\le
\left\lfloor
\frac{\binom{32}{t}R_r(d(8t))}
     {\binom{32-h}{t-h}}
\right\rfloor.
\]

The floor is valid before random mixing because (F_r(h)) is an integer.
`outer_hill_incidence.incidence_rank_caps` minimizes this bound over all
(t\ge h), intersects existing valid caps, and enforces monotonicity.
The numerator uses a per-subset dimension cap, never a cumulative cap.

The fresh interface is
`outer_hill_incidence.authenticated_bch_cdf(last_lp=104, refined=True)`.
It returns the transported rational CDF and explicit premises, including
the fresh H5 result. It does not accept a different generator as an input.

Tests: `python -m unittest test_outer_hill_incidence test_outer_hill_octets`
from this directory. Twelve tests pass, including exhaustive toy-code
rank counts and expected-CDF transport comparisons.

## Follow-up: packing six-octet supports

After the H5 exclusion, every word supported in at most six octets has
exactly six active octets. Each six-octet set supports at most one nonzero
word, because (d(48)=1).

Two distinct such words cannot have canonical supports sharing five
octets. Their union would fit seven octets and would span a subspace of
dimension two, contrary to (d(56)=1). Thus every five-octet set belongs
to at most one of these six-octet supports. If (A_6) counts the words,

\[
A_6\le\left\lfloor\frac{\binom{32}{5}}6\right\rfloor=33562.
\]

A recursive packing count gives 33461. Define (P(v,k,t)) to bound the
number of (k)-subsets whose (t)-subsets occur at most once. Counting
memberships at a point gives

\[
P(v,k,t)\le\left\lfloor\frac vk P(v-1,k-1,t-1)\right\rfloor,
\qquad P(v,k,0)=1.
\]

For ((v,k,t)=(32,6,5)), the successive bounds are
(14,135,1012,6274,33461). Hence (F_1(6)\le15\cdot33461), and
(F_r(6)=0) for (r\ge2). This improves the implemented incidence
codeword cap 97092 by 1.536871866 bits. It does not exclude H6.

No cyclic symmetry reduction was established. In particular, punctured
cyclic rotation by eight, with the parity coordinate fixed, takes 109 of
the 128 production generator rows outside (C). The intermediate
production subcode is not interchangeable with the surrounding cyclic
BCH codes, and the parity octet also breaks a simple octet rotation.

## Implemented Option: Intersecting Complements of High-Dimension Shortenings

For (j\le66), define a family of 12-subsets

\[
\mathcal A_j
=\{[32]\setminus T:|T|=20,\ d_T\ge j\}.
\]

First consider (\mathcal A_{66}). Suppose two members were disjoint.
The corresponding 20-octet sets (T,T') would intersect in eight octets.
Their shortened spaces satisfy

\[
C_T\cap C_{T'}=C_{T\cap T'},\qquad
\dim(C_T\cap C_{T'})\le d(64)=2.
\]

But two 66-dimensional subspaces of the 128-dimensional space (C)
have intersection dimension at least (66+66-128=4), a contradiction.
Thus (\mathcal A_{66}) is an intersecting family.

The Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem states that an intersecting family of
(k)-subsets of an (n)-set has at most \(\binom{n-1}{k-1}\) members
when (n\ge2k). Here (n=32\ge24=2k), so

\[
|\mathcal A_{66}|\le\binom{31}{11}
=\frac38\binom{32}{20}.
\]

This is the uniform-family case of Theorem 1 in Erdős, Ko, and Rado,
*Intersection theorems for systems of finite sets*, Quart. J. Math.
Oxford (2), 12 (1961), 313–320; its proof is in Section 5.
[Original paper](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1961-07.pdf).

Every remaining 20-octet shortening has dimension at most 65. Therefore
the total rank-(r) incidence count over those subsets is bounded by

\[
I_r^{\rm EKR}(20):=
\binom{32}{20}R_r(65)
+\binom{31}{11}\bigl(R_r(66)-R_r(65)\bigr).
\]

For every (h\le20), this yields the cap

\[
F_r(h)\le
\left\lfloor\frac{I_r^{\rm EKR}(20)}
{\binom{32-h}{20-h}}\right\rfloor.
\]

`outer_hill_intersection.authenticated_bch_cdf` checks production dimension
128 and the fresh uniform premises (d(64)\le2), (d(160)\le66). It intersects
this cap with the existing caps, preserving the H5 result and both the old
premises and the EKR step in a distinct record schema. It never reinterprets
the aggregate incidence bound as a dimension bound for each subset.
Seven focused tests pass; a separate review checked the argument and code.

### Possible cross-intersection refinement

The families (\mathcal A_{66}\subseteq\mathcal A_{65}) are also
cross-intersecting. Disjoint members from these two families would force
intersection dimension at least (66+65-128=3>2). This does not make
(\mathcal A_{65}) itself intersecting: two dimensions of 65 force only
intersection dimension two, which the current premise permits.

A further improvement would need a valid joint cardinality bound for
these nested, cross-intersecting families. In a layer-cake count, it would
control both

\[
|\mathcal A_{65}|\bigl(R_r(65)-R_r(64)\bigr)
\quad\text{and}\quad
|\mathcal A_{66}|\bigl(R_r(66)-R_r(65)\bigr).
\]

No such additional bound is claimed or implemented here. Applying EKR
separately to (\mathcal A_{65}) would be unjustified.

## Size of the local improvements

The following diagnostics use the retained dimension table in
`tmp/packed-closure/r2-d095-parallel-base-p256.json`. They are exact count
comparisons with floating log displays, not fresh whole-code replays.

The implemented incidence step saves 3.222392 bits at H6, 1.415037 at H7,
and 0.117836 at H8. It leaves H9 through H18 unchanged. At H19, H20, and
H21, it saves 3.700440, 0.392317, and 0.459432 bits respectively.
H22 through H32 are unchanged.

The optional EKR refinement saves a further 1.272079545 bits at H19 and
0.879762123 at H20. Its direct-shell gains are concentrated in a narrow
output-support range:

| Output support | H5 + incidence gain over old shells | Additional EKR gain |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 104 | 0.142376 | 0.007325 |
| 112 | 0.578003 | 0.035139 |
| 120 | 1.949302 | 0.217203 |
| 128 | 3.432638 | 0.940929 |
| 136 | 3.659617 | 1.252273 |
| 144 | 0.000743 | 0.000085 |

These shell improvements do not imply the same improvement per occupied
group in the final comparison. A new positive mixture and fresh inner
evaluation are required. The current low-mean dense obstruction may be
largely unaffected, because these refinements target other support ranges.

The first fresh dense probes with `hill_probe.py --outer-refinement
--intersection --quick` still give vacuous R2 bounds at 10% distance.
At means .104, .114, and .120, log2 upper bounds are approximately
+3050.60, +4294.65, and +4372.35. These adaptive searches are not a
matched-witness comparison with earlier runs; the local count improvement
does not guarantee that every search selects a better witness. The EKR
refinement is valid, but it has not closed the dense obstacle.

Recommended next step: retain EKR as an inexpensive count refinement while
investigating the remaining inner terms. H6 packing remains a lower-priority
sparse fallback; the R2 sparse prefix already exceeds 40 bits at 10%.
