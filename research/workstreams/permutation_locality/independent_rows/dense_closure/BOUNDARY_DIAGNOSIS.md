# Four-bit boundary diagnosis: retaining randomness in the shuffle comparison

The selected-composition diagnostics identify a removable loss in the
dense proof, not a demonstrated weakness of the encoder. At a 5% output
cutoff, four tested compositions fail the earlier comparison but pass a
sharper one by at least 420 bits. The code, maps, inner parameters, and
setup distribution are unchanged. The full certificate remains
[0.5% with more than 43.744 bits of margin](FIRST_CLOSURE.md).

## What was tested

The construction has K=2^20, N=2^21, 2048 four-row groups, 256 regions,
and two-update IMT(128,19). The [positive row envelope](README.md#comparison-measure)
has components with bit probabilities 0, 1, 1/2, 2/5, and 3/5, labeled
0 through 4. A group label lists four such components. For example,
`0003` contains three zero components and one Bernoulli(2/5) component;
it does not specify an actual BCH row weight. Unlisted groups are `0000`.

Each diagnostic includes the group's counting coefficients and the full
multinomial multiplicity of its locations among the 2048 groups. These
are terms in a positive comparison measure, not a partition into actual
BCH word types. Bounding a few terms does not bound their complete sum.

The following are approximate log2 upper bounds on those individual
contributions to the expected number of bad messages. The actual output
cutoffs are floor(delta N). Negative is useful; a positive number only
means this upper bound is too large, not that a bad word exists.

the first composition but also many others, and starts at occupancy 59
rather than 65. The contrast supports investigating whole-cell slack;
it does not prove that every composition in the cell passes pointwise.

## Why the earlier shuffle charge is excessive here

The general comparison bounds an arbitrary realized packet-category
count vector by independent packets. It pays for conditioning those
independent packets on exact counts. This works for every composition,
but does not retain the randomness in the counts already generated by
the product-row comparison components.

For the four failing 5% cases, this regional conditioning charge,
accumulated over 256 regions, exceeds the positive shortfall. Existing
count-dependent density tangents improve the bound but do not give a
useful margin for all four cases. A special-composition comparison can
retain the count randomness exactly:

| Composition | Earlier bound | Density tangent | Exact shuffle comparison |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 65 `0003` | +657.10 | +307.25 | -420.88 |
| 80 `0003` | +355.68 | -0.80 | -759.89 |
| 65 `2222` | +2698.97 | +1252.78 | -1539.54 |
| 64 `0003`, one `1111` | +477.39 | +124.15 | -597.88 |

The exact-comparison witnesses were independently replayed at 256-bit
precision. Each column optimizes its own witness parameters; the table
is not a subtraction of costs at a common tilt. For example, the total
shuffle charge for the first case drops from about 1104.77 bits in its
earlier witness to 26.63 bits in the new witness.

## Exact comparison for a homogeneous stochastic component

We first work within one region, after the positive category tilt used
in the dense proof. Fix n packet slots and q of them with the same
categorical distribution f=(f_0,...,f_4). Independently sample these q
categories, fill the other n-q slots with category zero, then uniformly
shuffle all n slots. Call this distribution P. Define Q to consist of
n independent categories with their common mean distribution

    x = (1-q/n) e_0 + (q/n) f.

Here P and Q are proof comparison distributions; neither is a newly
proposed code setup. Let v=1-f_0 and let A count nonzero categories.
Under P, A has distribution Bin(q,v). Under Q it has distribution
Bin(n,qv/n). Conditional on A=a, both distributions choose a uniform
a-element support and independently assign its categories with
probabilities f_j/v for j>0. Thus, for every sequence in P's support,
its pointwise likelihood ratio is

    R(a) = Pr[Bin(q,v)=a] / Pr[Bin(n,qv/n)=a].

For 0<q<n and 0<v<1, successive ratios satisfy

    R(a+1)/R(a) = (q-a)/(n-a) * (n-qv)/(q(1-v)).

This is at least one exactly when a<=qv. Consequently the maximum is
attained at a*=min(q,floor(qv)+1). If qv is an integer there can be two
adjacent maximizers. The cases q=0, q=n, or v=0 have loss one. For v=1,
only A=q occurs under P, and the loss is 1/Pr[Bin(n,q/n)=q].

Writing B=max_a R(a), we obtain P(y)<=B Q(y) pointwise. It follows that
E_P[F]<=B E_Q[F] for every nonnegative function F of the sequence.
Independent uniform packet-lane shuffles preserve this comparison on
four-bit packets: conditional on category j, both sides use the uniform
weight-j packet. The bound therefore applies to the existing inner
moment without an independence assumption about the inner state.

### Disjoint constant categories

We can also include fixed counts r_j of nonzero categories absent from
the stochastic law f. Set n'=n-sum_j r_j. Under the iid mean comparison,
the probability of getting exactly these counts in these categories is

    p_fixed = n!/(n'! product_j r_j!)
              * (n'/n)^n' * product_j (r_j/n)^r_j.

This event has positive probability. Conditioned on it, these fixed
categories occupy uniformly chosen positions, as under the shuffled
distribution. On the remaining n' positions the reference distribution
is iid with mean (1-q/n')e_0+(q/n')f. The exact maximum likelihood ratio
is therefore B(n',q,v)/p_fixed. This covers the composition with one
`1111` group and 64 `0003` groups. The implementation rejects overlap
with a constant category and rejects multiple distinct stochastic laws.

### Restoring the tilt and combining regions

For an original group law pi_i and positive tilt t, use
Z_i=sum_j pi_i(j)t_j and f_i(j)=pi_i(j)t_j/Z_i. Compute the preceding
comparison for these tilted laws. If x is their mean, undo the tilt
using unnormalized reference weights w_j=x_j/t_j. This retains the
existing factors product_i Z_i^(256 n_i) and (sum_j w_j)^524288.
The iid inner kernel uses probabilities w_j/sum_j w_j, not x_j.

The 256 regional comparison measures are independent before entering
the inner, so their pointwise losses multiply to B^256. This product
bound applies to any nonnegative function of the whole routed input,
including its output-weight moment averaged over the unchanged inner
setup. Outer counting coefficients and group-location multiplicities
are then included exactly as before.

## Implementation, verification, and next step

`shuffle_comparison.py` computes the maximum likelihood ratio with
exact rationals. The floating version only proposes witnesses;
`selected.py` rechecks rational witnesses with outward Arb arithmetic.
The tests exhaust all binomial counts for n<=12 and all categorical
count profiles in small examples, including two disjoint constant
categories. They compare the formula with the exact maximum, not only
with a floating approximation. All 23 dense-closure tests pass.

The next step is to extend this comparison to heterogeneous stochastic
laws and obtain bounds valid throughout cover cells. The subsequent
[posterior extension](POSTERIOR_COMPARISON.md) supplies the mixed-law
inequality and complete enumeration of small cells; broader coverage
remains open. Nearby mixtures
cannot inherit the selected bounds by interpolation. In particular,
means alone need not determine the likelihood-ratio loss. The general
comparison must either retain enough composition information or bound
all compositions in the cell uniformly. It can fall back to the
existing general inequality where that inequality already succeeds.

This is now a more concrete target than changing the inner or blindly
subdividing the atlas. It remains possible that other compositions
require a stronger inner bound. No larger full certificate is claimed.
