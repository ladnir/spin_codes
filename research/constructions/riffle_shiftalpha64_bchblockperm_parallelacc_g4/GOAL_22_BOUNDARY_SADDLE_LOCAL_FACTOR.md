# Goal 22: restore the local coefficient factor

## Result

The missing coefficient factor is quantitatively understood in the proof
gym. A face-aware saddle approximation recovers almost all of the loss from
positive-fugacity evaluation.

On the complete weight-16 boundary shell, the corrected aggregate differs
from the exact result by 0.25--0.27 bits at binary lengths 48 through 136.
On three proportionally scaled packet histograms, the corrected error is at
most 0.13 bits at scale four. The uncorrected loss grows at the rate predicted
by the dimension of the active histogram face.

These results are numerical evidence. The Gaussian local factor is not yet a
proved upper bound.

## Exact tilted identity

Fix a packet histogram $\mathbf h$ on the boundary $H=2D$. Let $S$ contain
the packet weights with positive counts. Set absent packet variables to zero.
Use $x_0=1$ to remove the common scale.

Define

\[
F_S(\boldsymbol\ell)
:=e_0^{\mathsf T}L_S(e^{\boldsymbol\ell})^N e_0,
\]

where $\boldsymbol\ell$ contains one log-fugacity for each nonzero element of
$S$. The active face has dimension

\[
d=|S|-1.
\]

The tilted law assigns each zero-to-zero boundary path probability
proportional to its transfer weight at $\boldsymbol\ell$. Let
$\mathbf K$ be the vector of its nonzero packet counts. Exact coefficient
extraction gives

\[
A_{N,4}(\mathbf h,D)
=F_S(\boldsymbol\ell)e^{-\langle\boldsymbol\ell,\mathbf h\rangle}
\Pr_{\boldsymbol\ell}[\mathbf K=\mathbf h].
\tag{1}
\]

The existing coefficient bound replaces the probability in (1) by one.
This replacement is the entire loss measured in Goal 21.

Choose a saddle $\boldsymbol\ell_*$ that satisfies

\[
\nabla\log F_S(\boldsymbol\ell_*)=\mathbf h.
\tag{2}
\]

The covariance matrix under the tilted law is

\[
\Sigma=\nabla^2\log F_S(\boldsymbol\ell_*).
\tag{3}
\]

Let $\nu_S$ be the index of the lattice generated by reachable histogram
differences on the active face. The tested local Gaussian factor is

\[
p_{\mathrm G}(\mathbf h)
:=
\frac{\nu_S}{(2\pi)^{d/2}\sqrt{\det\Sigma}}.
\tag{4}
\]

The lattice factor in (4) is necessary. The measured faces have indices two
and four. A parity-only factor would therefore be incorrect on some faces.

## Complete boundary-shell comparison

Every row uses $H=16$ and $D=8$. The raw loss compares exact extraction with
face-aware positive-fugacity evaluation. The corrected error compares the
exact outer-weighted shell with (1) after replacing its point probability by
(4).

| Binary length | Exact shell log | Raw coefficient loss | Corrected aggregate error |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 48 | -3.2558 | 4.7152 bits | 0.2504 bits |
| 80 | -3.1172 | 5.0881 bits | 0.2565 bits |
| 112 | -2.9842 | 5.1518 bits | 0.2646 bits |
| 136 | -2.8899 | 5.1598 bits | 0.2697 bits |

The first two exact shells reuse the Goal 21 receipts. The larger two shells
use direct integer extraction from the boundary matrix. The scaled GF(16)
construction permits at most fifteen distinct nonzero data coefficients, so
binary length 136 is the largest instance in this exact family.

The aggregate agreement does not imply a uniform per-type estimate. At
length 136, individual log-errors range from -1.114 to 2.016 bits. The outer
weights suppress the types with the largest errors.

## Proportional-scaling comparison

The fixed-weight experiment does not model the target regime, where $H$ can
grow with $N$. The second experiment scales three complete histograms. Their
base packet count is twelve.

| Face | Dimension | Raw loss at scale 1 | Raw loss at scale 4 | Corrected error at scale 4 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $(4,0,8,0,0)$ | 1 | 1.2762 | 2.2496 | 0.0112 bits |
| $(4,2,4,2,0)$ | 3 | 4.8660 | 7.9863 | 0.0598 bits |
| $(5,1,4,1,1)$ | 4 | 6.5472 | 9.8518 | 0.1302 bits |

Between scales two and four, the raw losses increase by 0.491, 1.492, and
2.046 bits. These increases closely match

\[
\frac d2\log_2(4/2)=\frac d2.
\]

Thus the raw coefficient bound loses the expected polynomial factor. The
Gaussian correction removes that growth on all three tested faces.

Every source coefficient in this experiment is an exact integer. The
saddles, covariance matrices, and corrected values use floating-point
arithmetic.

## Implication for full size

The experiment supports the boundary matrix as a source of tight full-size
estimates. It also confirms that the uncorrected coefficient bound is not
tight. On a four-dimensional interior face, the omitted factor has order
$N^{-2}$ under the local-limit model. This corresponds to about 38 bits when
$N\approx2^{19}$, before constants.

The correction itself is inexpensive. It requires a saddle point, a
$4\times4$ covariance matrix, and the lattice index of the active face. The
remaining issue is certification.

## Proposed proof step

Equation (1) reduces certification to an upper bound on one point probability
of a finite-state Markov additive process. Fourier inversion gives the
appropriate route. The characteristic function is a ratio of two
$5\times5$ transfer expressions:

\[
\phi(\boldsymbol\theta)
=
\frac{F_S(\boldsymbol\ell_*+i\boldsymbol\theta)}
{F_S(\boldsymbol\ell_*)}.
\tag{5}
\]

A certified proof can divide the Fourier domain into two regions.

1. Near the dual lattice, bound (5) by a Gaussian using the Hessian and a
   controlled third derivative.
2. Away from the dual lattice, bound the spectral radius of the complex
   transfer matrix below its value at the saddle.

The construction has five states and at most sixteen active-face patterns.
Parameter boxes can cover the saddle range needed by the outer proof. This
turns the next task into a finite-dimensional analytic certificate rather
than a new enumeration over all packet histograms.
