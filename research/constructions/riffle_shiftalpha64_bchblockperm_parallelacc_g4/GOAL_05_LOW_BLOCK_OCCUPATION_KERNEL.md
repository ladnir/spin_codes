# Goal 05: low-block occupation kernel

## Question

Which random event makes the packet accumulator produce low output? A useful
answer must retain the local block grouping, the order of packet values, and
the global gaps in one probability space.

For a fixed message, let

\[
\mathbf h=(h_1,\ldots,h_s)
\]

be the weights of its nonzero BCH blocks. Independent local permutations make
block $i$ a uniform weight-$h_i$ slice in 128 bits. Define

\[
K_D(\mathbf h)
:=
\Pr_{\boldsymbol\sigma,\Pi}
\left[\sum_{t=1}^{N}\operatorname{wt}(S_t)\le D
\ \middle|\ \mathbf h\right].
\tag{1}
\]

The complete first moment has the exact form

\[
\mathbb E Z_D
=
\sum_{\mathbf h}A_{\mathrm{outer}}(\mathbf h)K_D(\mathbf h).
\tag{2}
\]

Equation (2), rather than a uniform fixed-word contraction, is the intended
interface between the outer code and the packet accumulator.

## Kernel experiment

The new analysis accepts $s$ equal-weight BCH blocks. Its rigorous part
pools their $128s$ coordinates into one uniform slice. It then conditions on
every labeled part having the declared weight. The conditioning step gives a
valid upper bound for the real independent-block law.

The 16-state dynamic program retains the prefix state in
$\mathbb F_2^4$. It sums every zero-state gap without cost and uses the actual
Hamming weight of each positive state. A separate numerical experiment
samples the real block law and the full global packet placement directly.

At binary output threshold $D=188{,}766$, the calculations give:

| Profile | balance cost | rigorous positive-state bound | mean $H$ | mean $m_0$ | sampled 0.1% output quantile |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $(24,24,24)$ | 6.1584 bits | $2^{-40.2950}$ | 54.609 | 3.4515 | $0.3199L$ |
| $(22,22,22,22)$ | 9.2795 bits | $2^{-44.6721}$ | 68.388 | 4.3525 | $0.3435L$ |
| $(64,64,64,64)$ | not run | numerical only | 120.4578 | 7.4852 | $0.3991L$ |

The first two rigorous runs reproduce the three-block certificate and extend
it to four blocks. The four-block minimum-weight profile gains 4.3771
probability bits over the three-block shifted minimum profile.

The numerical sample sizes are 2,000 for each low-weight profile and 5,000 for
the weight-64 profile. No sample produced output below $0.09L$. These samples
describe typical behavior only. They do not certify a rare-event probability.

## Observed mechanism

The active-state walk is close to uniform on $\mathbb F_2^4$ after a short
initial segment. Three independent checks support this interpretation.

1. The observed mean number of zero states is close to $H/16$.
2. The observed sum of prefix-state weights is close to $2H$.
3. Final states have even Hamming weight, as required by the extended-BCH
   parity, but intermediate states use all 16 values.

Packet support is therefore not the observed obstruction. The rigorous
positive-state sums are dominated by a different, tilted law:

| Profile | typical $m_0$ | dominant tilted $H$ | dominant tilted $m_0$ |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| $(24,24,24)$ | 3.45 | 53--57 | 17--20 |
| $(22,22,22,22)$ | 4.35 | 68--72 | 21--25 |

Thus the low-output event keeps nearly typical packet support but increases
the zero-state occupation from about $H/16$ to about $H/3$. Long global
gaps can then fall on those zero states. This joint return-and-gap deviation
is the true low-block mechanism exposed by the current calculation.

The word *dominant* refers to the largest terms in the rigorous exponential
sum. It does not assert that the Chernoff bound is tight.

## Consequence for the proof strategy

The earlier profile sweep maximized over state histograms before charging the
local randomization. That order admitted an unattainable $w=16,H=4$
bottleneck and erased the cost of excessive returns.

Equation (2) suggests the opposite order. For packet value $v$, define the
16-state tilted transition

\[
T_v(z)_{q,q+v}:=z^{\operatorname{wt}(q+v)}.
\]

Local weight conditioning can be represented by

\[
M(x,z):=\sum_{v\in\mathbb F_2^4}
x^{\operatorname{wt}(v)}T_v(z).
\tag{3}
\]

Coefficient extraction in $x$ enforces a BCH-block weight. Products of the
matrices in (3), symmetrized over the global packet order, retain the complete
return statistic. The scalar accumulator enumerator is the two-state instance
of the same construction.

The next lemma should bound the tilted return partition function generated by
(3). It should charge the large-deviation event $m_0\approx H/3$ before the
outer spectrum is summed. A successful lemma would replace low-block
profile-by-profile casework with one 16-state spectral calculation.
