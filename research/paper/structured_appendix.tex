\section{Asymptotic Structured SPIN Certificate}

\subsection{Certificate for the Scalable Family}
\label{app:structured-scalable-certificate}

This appendix supplies the detailed estimates for the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:structured-spin-scalable} in
Section~\ref{sec:structured-distance}. The argument
first selects one Golay--BA-3 constituent whose realized spectrum obeys a
certified envelope. It then reduces the structured route to occupation and
mean row weight. The final first-moment bound separates four regimes:
$Q=1,2$, fixed $Q\ge3$, growing sparse $Q$, and positive relative
occupation. Finite IMT transfers handle the last two regimes. Continuum
transfers handle the fixed regimes.

For $0\le d\le N$ and $1\le Q\le L$, let $Z_{d,Q}$ count nonzero messages
with exactly $Q$ active outer blocks and encoded weight at most $d$. Unless a
display states otherwise, expectations and probabilities include the shared
BA constituent, the route permutations, and the IMT transvections. The
finite-length specialization is not part of this probability space.

\subsubsection{The One-Sampled Golay--BA-3 Outer}

We begin with the exact expected spectrum before selecting the shared
constituent. Let
\[
  G(u):=1+759u^8+2576u^{12}+759u^{16}+u^{24}
\]
be the weight polynomial of the extended Golay code, and define
\[
  G_b(a):=[u^a]G(u)^{b/24}.
\]
For $a\ge1$, put $r_a:=\lceil a/2\rceil$ and
\begin{equation}
  T_b(a,c)
  :=
  \binom{c-1}{r_a-1}\binom{b-c}{a-r_a},
  \qquad
  P_b(a,c):=\frac{T_b(a,c)}{\binom ba}.
  \label{eq:structured-acc-transition}
\end{equation}
We set $T_b(0,0):=1$ and $T_b(0,c):=0$ for $c\ne0$. Binomial coefficients
outside their natural range are zero. Thus $P_b(a,c)$ is the exact
weight-transition probability of a uniformly interleaved accumulator.

For a realized BA constituent $\mathcal O_b$, let
$A_{\mathcal O_b}(w)$ count its nonzero words of weight $w$, and define
\[
  \overline A_b(w)
  :=
  \E_{\tau_1,\tau_2}[A_{\mathcal O_b}(w)].
\]

\begin{lemma}[Exact expected BA spectrum]
\label{lem:structured-exact-ba-spectrum}
For every $0\le w\le b$,
\begin{equation}
  \overline A_b(w)
  =
  \sum_{a=1}^b\sum_{c=0}^b
  G_b(a)P_b(a,c)P_b(c,w).
  \label{eq:structured-exact-ba-spectrum}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix a Golay direct-sum word of weight $a$. The first uniform permutation and
accumulator produce weight $c$ with probability $P_b(a,c)$. Conditioned on
that weight, the second permutation makes the word uniform on its Hamming
slice. The second accumulator then produces weight $w$ with probability
$P_b(c,w)$. Summing over the $G_b(a)$ input words proves
\eqref{eq:structured-exact-ba-spectrum}.
\end{proof}

We use this formula to bound both tails and the central spectrum.
The tails require a separate estimate for messages supported on few Golay
blocks; a limiting exponent alone does not control those messages. Let
\[
  h(x):=-x\ln x-(1-x)\ln(1-x)
\]
with its continuous endpoint values. Define
\[
  g(a)
  :=
  \inf_{u>0}\left\{\frac1{24}\ln G(u)-a\ln u\right\}.
\]
On the accumulator feasibility region $a/2\le c\le1-a/2$, set
\[
  \pi(a,c)
  :=
  c h\!\left(\frac{a}{2c}\right)
  +(1-c)h\!\left(\frac{a}{2(1-c)}\right)-h(a),
\]
and set $\pi(a,c):=-\infty$ outside that region. The limiting exponent is
\begin{equation}
  a_{\rm BA}(x)
  :=
  \sup_{0\le a,c\le1}
  \{g(a)+\pi(a,c)+\pi(c,x)\}.
  \label{eq:structured-ba-variational}
\end{equation}
Uniform coefficient and Stirling bounds in
\eqref{eq:structured-exact-ba-spectrum} give
\begin{equation}
  \frac1b\ln\overline A_b(w)
  \le
  a_{\rm BA}(w/b)
  +O\!\left(\frac{\ln b}{b}\right).
  \label{eq:structured-ba-asymptotic}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[BA outer tails]
\label{lem:structured-ba-tails}
As $b\to\infty$ through multiples of $24$,
\[
  \sum_{w/b\notin[0.104,0.896]}\overline A_b(w)=o(1).
\]
The expectation is over the two independent BA permutations.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $\delta_o:=13/125$ and $\eta:=1/1000$. We split nonzero messages
by the number $j$ of active Golay blocks. There are at most
\[
  N_j:=\binom{b/24}{j}4095^j
  \le\left(\frac{4095eb}{24j}\right)^j
\]
such messages. Their input weight to the first accumulator is even,
say $a=2\ell$, with $4j\le\ell\le12j$.

First consider $j\le\eta b$. For a uniform weight-$v$ input $V$,
the transition formula \eqref{eq:structured-acc-transition} bounds the
lower-tail probability by
\begin{equation}
  p_v:=\sum_{0\le h\le\delta_o b}P_b(v,h)
  \le c_o^{\lfloor v/2\rfloor},
  \qquad c_o:=\frac{4\delta_o}{1-\delta_o}=\frac{13}{28}.
  \label{eq:structured-ba-one-acc-tail}
\end{equation}
Indeed, for $v=2\ell$ and $D:=\lfloor\delta_o b\rfloor$, summing
$\binom{h-1}{\ell-1}$ over $h\le D$ gives
\[
  p_{2\ell}
  \le\frac{\binom b\ell\binom D\ell}{\binom b{2\ell}}
  =\binom{2\ell}{\ell}\frac{\binom D\ell}{\binom{b-\ell}\ell}
  \le c_o^\ell.
\]
If $\ell>D$, the probability is zero. The odd case has an additional
factor at most $2\delta_o/(1-\delta_o)<1$. The bound also holds for $v=0$.

Set $\zeta:=\sqrt{c_o}$ and $\kappa:=4\zeta/(1-\zeta)$.
Let $H_1,H_2$ be the weights after the first and second accumulators.
Conditional on $H_1$, the second permutation makes its input uniform;
thus \eqref{eq:structured-ba-one-acc-tail} bounds the lower-tail
probability by $\zeta^{-1}\E[\zeta^{H_1}]$.
For a fixed first input of weight $2\ell$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \E[\zeta^{H_1}]
  &\le\frac{\binom b\ell}{\binom b{2\ell}}
      \sum_{h\ge\ell}\binom{h-1}{\ell-1}\zeta^h\\
  &=\frac{\binom b\ell}{\binom b{2\ell}}
      \left(\frac{\zeta}{1-\zeta}\right)^\ell
  \le\left(\frac{\kappa\ell}{b-2\ell+1}\right)^\ell.
\end{split}
\label{eq:structured-ba-sparse-moment}
\end{equation}
For $\ell/b\le12\eta$, the last expression decreases with $\ell$.
To check this, its logarithmic derivative is at most
$\ln(\kappa x/(1-2x))+1+2x/(1-2x)$ at $x=12\eta$,
which is below $-1.22$.
Consequently, every message with $j\le\eta b$ satisfies
\[
  \E[\zeta^{H_1}]
  \le\left(\frac{4\kappa j}{b(1-8\eta)}\right)^{4j}.
\]
Summing over these messages gives
\begin{equation}
  S_b:=\sum_{1\le j\le\eta b}
    \left[C_*\left(\frac jb\right)^3\right]^j,
  \qquad
  C_*:=\frac{4095e}{24}
       \left(\frac{4\kappa}{1-8\eta}\right)^4.
  \label{eq:structured-ba-sparse-sum}
\end{equation}
The expected number of sparse lower-tail words is at most
$\zeta^{-1}S_b$. Outward interval arithmetic gives
$C_*\eta^3<0.657$. For $j\le\sqrt b$, the summands are bounded by
a geometric series with base $C_*b^{-3/2}$; the remaining summands
are at most $0.657^j$. Hence $S_b=O(b^{-3/2})$.

The sparse upper tail needs an explicit bound as well. Let
$e_1=(1,0,\ldots,0)$. Since
$\operatorname{Acc}(V)+\mathbf1=\operatorname{Acc}(V+e_1)$,
conditioning on the first bit of a uniform weight-$v$ input gives
\[
 \Prob[\operatorname{wt}(\operatorname{Acc}(V))\ge(1-\delta_o)b]
 \le \frac{v}{b-v+1}p_{v-1}
      +\frac{b-v}{v+1}p_{v+1}.
\]
At $v=b$ the second term is absent. Each prefactor is at most $b$.
Using \eqref{eq:structured-ba-one-acc-tail}, the right side is at most
$b(\zeta^{-2}+1)\zeta^v$ for $v\ge1$.
Apply this bound with $v=H_1$, which is nonzero for a nonzero input.
Equation~\eqref{eq:structured-ba-sparse-sum} bounds the expected number
of sparse upper-tail words by $b(\zeta^{-2}+1)S_b=O(b^{-1/2})=o(1)$.

It remains to bound messages with $j>\eta b$. Write
$\alpha:=a/b$, $\beta:=H_1/b$, and $x:=H_2/b$.
Then $\alpha\ge8\eta=0.008$. Accumulator support constraints imply
$x\ge\alpha/4\ge0.002$. For the lower tail, the interval verifier proves
\begin{equation}
  g(\alpha)+\pi(\alpha,\beta)+\pi(\beta,x)
  <-7.68\cdot10^{-8}
  \label{eq:structured-ba-dense-tail}
\end{equation}
at every feasible point with
$\alpha\in[0.008,1]$, $\beta\in[0,1]$, and $x\in[0.002,0.104]$.
The branch-and-bound check processes $6{,}747$ boxes and leaves no
unresolved boxes. Uniform coefficient and Stirling bounds apply to
each summand of \eqref{eq:structured-exact-ba-spectrum}; the polynomial
number of weights and the $O(\ln b)$ remainder preserve exponential decay.
This argument restricts the initial weight before taking a supremum:
the unrestricted exponent $a_{\rm BA}$ also includes sparse paths.

For the dense upper tail, reflect $x$ to $1-x$ and use
$\pi(\beta,x)=\pi(\beta,1-x)$.
The finite support constraints can differ from the limiting feasibility
region by $O(1/b)$ because of parity. The entropy terms are uniformly
continuous, so these shifts contribute $o(1)$ to the normalized exponent.
The strict negative gap in \eqref{eq:structured-ba-dense-tail} therefore
also gives exponential decay for the dense upper tail.
Combining the sparse and dense estimates proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

Our verification code checks the monotonicity and geometric constants,
and uses interval arithmetic to check \eqref{eq:structured-ba-dense-tail}.
The author-side paper-to-code map gives the sources and replay commands.
Lemma~\ref{lem:structured-one-sample-selection} now selects the shared
outer. We next bound its spectrum on $\mathcal W=[0.104,0.896]$.

The certificate represents a concave majorant $\widehat a_{\rm BA}$ as the
lower envelope of rational affine supports. It proves
\begin{equation}
  a_{\rm BA}(x)\le\widehat a_{\rm BA}(x)
  \qquad(x\in\mathcal W).
  \label{eq:ba-spectrum-majorant}
\end{equation}
The refined majorant has $39$ affine segments. It retains the old supports
and adds five left-half supports with slopes $0.09,0.07,0.05,0.03,0.01$,
together with their reflections. Each new intercept is an outward rational
upper bound on $\ln(1+e^{-s})-(\ln2)/2+10^{-5}$ for its slope $s$.
This expression proposes the line; validity follows from outward checks of
\eqref{eq:structured-ba-variational} over its entire active interval.
The refinement accepts $5{,}363$ leaves after $10{,}721$ box checks, with
largest upper residual below $-1.70\cdot10^{-9}$. Exact partition replay
and higher working precision confirm every leaf. Reflection uses
$\pi(c,x)=\pi(c,1-x)$, not a symmetry of each realized constituent.
Inherited intervals only shrink. The central support is an outward rational
rounding of the exact code-size bound $(\ln2)/2$. Taking the lower envelope
preserves concavity.
Equations~\eqref{eq:structured-ba-asymptotic} and
\eqref{eq:ba-spectrum-majorant} give the outer exponent used below. The
verification records retain all affine supports and box witnesses.
