\section{Turbo SPIN: Fixed Outer and Four-Bit Packets}
\label{sec:turbo-spin}

Scalable SPIN uses one-bit packets and a growing shared outer to obtain
an asymptotic guarantee. For selected finite lengths, \emph{Turbo SPIN}
uses a short RS outer and routes four-bit packets. It retains the same
recursive update law and the same fixed state map. Its distance proof
keeps packet support and values where the one-bit proof uses row
weights. The asymptotic theorem of Section~\ref{sec:structured-distance}
does not apply to this route.

\paragraph{Construction.}
Fix $K=2^e$ with $e\in\{14,16,18,20,22,24,26\}$, set
$(k,b,p,t)=(128,256,4,64)$, $L=K/128$, and $N=2K$. Interpret each local
input group as eight rows of four
symbols over $\F_{16}$. Encode each row by the same systematic MDS
RS$[8,4]$ code. The eight symbols at one code position form a $32$-bit
column. For each group and each of its eight columns, independently sample
a nonzero element of $\F_{2^{32}}$ and apply the binary adjoint of
multiplication by that element. This orientation makes the transposed
encoder apply field multiplication directly. The column maps are part of
the sampled code and remain fixed for every message.
The RS rows and column maps together form the sampled local map $G_j$
in Definition~\ref{def:structured-spin}; these maps are independent
across groups.

Split each randomized column into eight four-bit packets. Apply the
packet route of Section~\ref{sec:regional-spin}: shuffle each group's
$B=64$ packets into distinct regions, then independently shuffle the $L$
packet slots of each region. Serialize in region-major order.
The inner uses recurrence~\eqref{eq:structured-inner} with $C=A^\top$.
At every length, the fixed map $A:\F_2^{22}\to\F_2^{64}$ is the $s=22$
map used by Scalable SPIN, and each step samples an independent uniform
$M_i\in\mathrm{GL}(22,2)$.
The state continues across region boundaries and is discarded after
the last output. Outer coefficients, both sets of
permutations, and state updates are mutually independent in the ideal
setup distribution. Appendix~\ref{app:shared-inner-map} specifies the
shared map; Appendix~\ref{app:turbo-proof} explains the finite proof
calculation.

\begin{theorem}[Finite Turbo SPIN distance]
\label{thm:turbo-spin}
For each listed $K$, every setup defines an injective binary linear
$[2K,K]$ encoder. Let $H=\lfloor N/10\rfloor$. Over the ideal setup
distribution just defined,
\[
  \Prob_\theta\bigl[\exists x\ne0:\wt(E_\theta(x))\le H\bigr]
  <2^{-\lambda_K},
\]
where $\lambda_K$ may be taken from Table~\ref{tab:turbo-margins}.
In particular, at every listed length the minimum distance exceeds $N/10$
except with setup probability below $2^{-52}$.
\end{theorem}

The outer is injective, and Lemma~\ref{lem:structured-inner-bijective}
gives the rate for every setup. For distance, independent column
randomization makes every fixed nonzero column uniform over the nonzero
$32$-bit vectors. This gives an exact expected packet-weight enumerator.
Lemma~\ref{lem:packet-route-symmetry} then removes dependence on the
original packet positions while retaining their four-bit values.
The first-moment calculation treats one and two active groups with their
support information intact. For three or more, a pointwise bound on the
outer measure transfers the existing regional inner bounds.
Appendix~\ref{app:turbo-proof} explains the calculation. At every listed
length, a Lean development checks the complete distance bound, including
every numerical certificate; it is the authority for
Theorem~\ref{thm:turbo-spin}.

A smaller state is also workable. A paired $15$-state map encodes about
$14\%$ faster at $K\le2^{16}$, and a numerical check gives it a margin of
$61.88$ bits at $K=2^{16}$, but we do not formalize it and use $s=22$
throughout.

The fixed $128$-bit local outer and four-bit packet route give no
asymptotic guarantee here. Scalable SPIN instead increases its outer
length with $N$ and uses a bit-level route.
