\section{Certified finite evaluation}
\label{sec:certificates}

The asymptotic theorems suppress polynomial factors and do not give a failure
probability at one concrete length.  Our finite certificates retain those
factors and cover every nonzero message support.  This section states the
largest results and explains what the verifier checks.
We call $-\log_2 U$ the \emph{failure exponent in bits}: a larger value means
a smaller upper bound $U$ on bad sampling.

\subsection{Verifier model}

A certificate contains integer parameters, a partition of the support range,
and decimal positive markers.  A floating-point optimizer proposes the
markers.  The verifier parses each decimal as an exact input and recomputes
the resulting expressions with Arb ball arithmetic through
\texttt{python-flint}.  A ball is a numerical interval together with a stated
error radius; every computed ball encloses the exact real value.

The verifier checks four conditions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item the stated support intervals are ordered, disjoint, and cover the
  required range;
  \item every marker belongs to the domain required by its inequality;
  \item each matrix, coefficient bound, and Hamming-ball bound is evaluated
  with outward rounding;
  \item the outward upper endpoint of the complete sum is below the claimed
  failure probability.
\end{enumerate}

The optimizer is outside the trusted base.  A poor marker only weakens a
bound.  The verification cache is also outside the trusted base: its key
includes the certificate, verifier source, precision, and
\texttt{python-flint} version.  A full run can disable the cache.

\begin{proposition}[Certificate soundness]
\label{prop:certificate-soundness}
Suppose a verifier evaluates a proved first-moment upper bound, checks a
partition of every support in $[1,k]$, and returns an Arb interval with upper
endpoint $U$.  Then the sampled ensemble satisfies
$\Pr[\Bad_L(\G)]\leq U$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each exact or relaxed support contribution is an upper bound by the theorem
implemented for that interval.  Outward rounding preserves every inequality.
The checked partition makes the sum cover all nonzero message supports
exactly once.  The relevant first-moment theorem then gives the probability
bound.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Binary two-sided regular EC}

The two-sided regular verifier proves
\eqref{eq:biregular-binary-ec-certificate}.  Exact regional transfers cover
supports $1$ through $16$.  The verifier uses $111$ convex coefficient
blocks outside the central range and $47$ local-limit blocks inside it.  A
separate transfer checks the all-one message.  At 192-bit precision, the
outward-rounded contributions are at most
\begin{align*}
 U_{\mathrm{exact}}&=9.436470\cdot10^{-16},\\
 U_{\mathrm{outer}}&=1.633667\cdot10^{-10},\\
 U_{\mathrm{central}}&=1.382398\cdot10^{-12},\\
 U_{\mathrm{full}}&<10^{-341856}.
\end{align*}
Their sum is below $1.6475000\cdot10^{-10}$, which gives $32.4990$ failure
bits.  The outer range determines the final bound, not the central
local-limit estimate.

The degree-$6/3$, memory-$79$ verifier proves
\eqref{eq:biregular-binary-ec-degree-six-certificate}.  Exact regional

are at most
\begin{align*}
 U_{\mathrm{exact}}&=2.829174\cdot10^{-7},\\
 U_{\mathrm{outer}}&=7.292690\cdot10^{-8},\\
 U_{\mathrm{central}}&=1.397617\cdot10^{-13},\\
 U_{\mathrm{full}}&<10^{-315674}.
\end{align*}
Their sum is below $3.558444\cdot10^{-7}$, which gives $21.4222$ failure
bits.  The largest exact block covers supports $112$ through $127$ and is
below $1.229988\cdot10^{-7}$.

The last exact block requires more dynamic range than one binary64 scale can
provide.  The verifier writes each nonnegative transfer as a sum of exponent
layers.  Entries in one layer share a dense mantissa matrix and a power-of-two
scale.  Matrix multiplication inflates each positive dot product upward.
Separate layers retain small reachable entries without introducing an
absolute error into structural zeros.  Arb combines the scaled row sums with
the message counts and output markers.

A checked-in JSON file for each profile fixes every positive marker and every
block endpoint.
A separate checker uses only integer arithmetic and decimal parsing from the
Python standard library.  It checks the parameter identities, marker domains,
and a disjoint cover of supports $1$ through $k$.  The numerical verifier runs
this checker before evaluating the stored markers.  It does not invoke the
floating-point optimizer during verification.

\subsection{EA over a 127-bit prime field}

The two-sided regular trace in Theorem~\ref{thm:p-ea-biregular-trace} closes a
large-field EA result at the exact floored GV cutoff.

\begin{theorem}[Finite large-field EA certificate]
\label{thm:p-ea-certificate}
Let $p=2^{127}-1$, $d_L=30$, $d_R=15$, $\ell=69{,}905$,
$n=2{,}097{,}150$, and $k=1{,}048{,}575$.  Let
\begin{equation}
 L=1{,}032{,}064
 =\left\lfloor n\delta_{p,\mathrm{GV}}(1/2)\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
For the labeled two-sided regular EA ensemble,
\begin{equation}
 \Pr_{\B}[\Bad_L(\B\A)]
 <9.499\cdot10^{-10}<2^{-29.97}.
 \label{eq:p-ea-certificate}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The verifier evaluates support sizes $1$ through $64$ with the exact regional
trace.  Twelve contiguous blocks cover $65\leq r<k$; a proved convexity
argument bounds every support inside a block from stored endpoint data.  Full
support is checked directly.  At 192-bit precision, the three contributions are below
$7.319\cdot10^{-30}$, $9.499\cdot10^{-10}$, and
$1.036\cdot10^{-75}$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate-soundness} completes
the proof.
\end{proof}

The degree is controlled by a dense-support term.  This is why left
regularity alone did not improve the large-field EA parameters in our scan.
Two-sided regularity prevents right coordinates from being entirely empty at
full message support.

\subsection{EC over fields near 128 bits}

The EC certificates combine the trace bound in
Theorem~\ref{thm:p-ec-trace}, the singleton refinement, positive-marker
coefficient bounds, and direct full-support evaluation.
We first give a degree--memory frontier over the 127-bit prime field
$\F_{2^{127}-1}$.  We then give two profiles over the 128-bit extension field
$\F_{2^{128}}$.  These are separate finite experiments: the fields,
parameters, cutoffs, and sampled generators are fixed independently.

\begin{theorem}[Finite $\F_{2^{127}-1}$ EC certificates]
\label{thm:p-ec-certificates}
Fix $p=2^{127}-1$ and rate $1/2$.  For every row of
Table~\ref{tab:p-ec-frontier} labeled EC, take $d_L$, $d_R$, $n$, $L$, and
$m$ from that row, and let $\ell=n/d_L$ and $k=n/2$.  Independently sample
the labeled two-sided regular expander and a finite-field convolution of
memory $m$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 \Pr_{\B,\C}[\Bad_L(\B\C)]<2^{-b},
\end{equation}
where $b$ is the displayed failure exponent.
\end{theorem}

It therefore isolates field-size scaling from the degree--memory tradeoff.
Define
\[

 {2{,}097{,}144\,\delta_{2^{128},\mathrm{GV}}(1/2)}.
\]

 L=\left\lfloor \eta n\delta_{p,\mathrm{GV}}(1/2)\right\rfloor.

The first row of Table~\ref{tab:p-ec-frontier} records EA as a memory-one
recursive map because the accumulator retains $y_{t-1}$.  It is not the
memory-one instance of \eqref{eq:prime-convolution}: EA fixes the feedback
coefficient to one, whereas finite-field EC samples it from $\F_p$.

Among the EC rows, memory $4$ and memory $6$ minimize the simple work proxy
$d_L+m$, both with value $30$.  Increasing memory to $12$ reduces the
expander degree to $22$, but raises the proxy to $34$.  Thus a larger
convolution helps the sparse regime, with diminishing returns.  The
diagnostic memory-two EC point also has left degree $30$.  It does not improve
the expander degree over certified EA and requires additional random field
arithmetic.

The proxy $d_L+m$ counts edges and retained field elements equally.  It does
not count the multiplications needed for random edge labels or feedback taps.
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} therefore treats it only as a guide for selecting
profiles to benchmark.

The present analysis does not certify degree $20$ with memory $21$.  At
support $512$, the exact structural upper bound is $2^{131.90}$.  A positive
upper bound is inconclusive; it is not evidence that the sampled ensemble has
a low-weight codeword.

The diagnostic points $(m,d_L)=(1,38)$ and $(2,30)$ remain outside the
certified frontier.  Neither improves the implementation-facing choice:
certified EA already uses degree $30$ with a fixed memory-one recurrence, and
the memory-two EC diagnostic has only a narrow floating-point margin.  We do
not use either point in a theorem or parameter recommendation.
