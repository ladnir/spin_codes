\section{Additional binary expander ensembles}
\label{app:additional-ensembles}

The main text uses independent entries and region-stratified regular rows.
This appendix records two intermediate choices.  They are useful for comparing
the effect of fixing a row or column weight while keeping the rest of the
sampling rule unchanged.

\subsection{Fixed column degree}

In the fixed-column ensemble, each of the $n$ columns is sampled independently
and uniformly from the binary vectors of length $k$ and weight $d$.  Fix a
message whose support has size $r$, and define
\begin{align}
 O_{r,d}&:=\sum_{b\geq0}\binom{r}{2b+1}\binom{k-r}{d-2b-1},&
 Z_{r,d}&:=\sum_{b\geq0}\binom{r}{2b}\binom{k-r}{d-2b}.
 \label{eq:fixed-column-counts}
\end{align}
The first quantity counts columns that meet the message support an odd number
of times; the second counts those that meet it an even number of times.  Thus
$O_{r,d}+Z_{r,d}=\binom{k}{d}$.

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-column enumerator]
\label{lem:fixed-column-enumerator}
For $0\leq u\leq n$, the expected number of weight-$r$ messages whose
expanded word has weight $u$ is
\begin{equation}
 \binom{k}{r}\binom{n}{u}
 \left(\frac{O_{r,d}}{\binom{k}{d}}\right)^u
 \left(\frac{Z_{r,d}}{\binom{k}{d}}\right)^{n-u}.
 \label{eq:fixed-column-enumerator}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed message, one output coordinate is nonzero exactly when the sampled
column has odd intersection with the message support.  This happens with
probability $O_{r,d}/\binom{k}{d}$.  The output coordinates are independent,
so their weight is binomially distributed.  The factor $\binom{k}{r}$ counts
the messages of the stated weight.
\end{proof}

The denominators in \eqref{eq:fixed-column-enumerator} are essential: without
them the expression counts matrices instead of averaging over the ensemble.

\subsection{Fixed row weight}

In the fixed-row ensemble, the $k$ rows are independent and uniform among the
binary vectors of length $n$ and weight $d$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 K_a^{(n)}(j):=\sum_{t\in\mathbb Z}(-1)^t
   \binom jt\binom{n-j}{a-t}.
 \label{eq:krawtchouk-definition}
\end{equation}
This polynomial is the signed difference between the number of weight-$a$
vectors that have even and odd intersection with a fixed $j$-coordinate set.
It is often called a binary Krawtchouk polynomial.

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-row enumerator]
\label{lem:fixed-row-enumerator}
For $0\leq u\leq n$, the expected number of weight-$r$ messages whose
expanded word has weight $u$ is
\begin{equation}
 \binom{k}{r}\frac{\binom{n}{u}}{2^n\binom{n}{d}^{r}}
 \sum_{j=0}^{n}K_d^{(n)}(j)^rK_j^{(n)}(u).
 \label{eq:fixed-row-enumerator}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed message, the expanded word is the binary sum of $r$ independent
uniform weight-$d$ rows.  The parity character indexed by a weight-$j$ vector
has expected value $K_d^{(n)}(j)/\binom nd$ on one row.  Fourier inversion on
the binary vector space gives the probability of each weight-$u$ output.
Multiplying by the $\binom nu$ outputs and $\binom kr$ messages gives
\eqref{eq:fixed-row-enumerator}.
\end{proof}

The alternating sum in \eqref{eq:fixed-row-enumerator} is exact but poorly
conditioned for numerical work.  The following recurrence uses only
nonnegative values.  Let $\rho_{0,0}=1$ and $\rho_{0,u}=0$ for $u>0$.  If the
current sum has weight $u$, adding a uniform weight-$d$ row gives weight
$v=u+d-2j$, where $j$ is the size of the intersection.  Therefore set
\begin{align}
 P_d(u,v)&:=\frac{\binom uj\binom{n-u}{d-j}}{\binom nd},
 &j&:=\frac{u+d-v}{2},
 \label{eq:fixed-row-transition}\\
 \rho_{r+1,v}&:=\sum_{u=0}^{n}\rho_{r,u}P_d(u,v).
 \label{eq:fixed-row-recurrence}
\end{align}
The transition is zero unless $j$ is an integer in the feasible intersection
range.  Then $\rho_{r,u}$ is the probability that a fixed weight-$r$ message
expands to weight $u$, and its expected shell count is
$\binom kr\rho_{r,u}$.

For larger message weights, one may instead compare a weight-$d$ row with an
independent-entry row of density $d/n$.  Conditioning the latter to have
weight $d$ costs
\begin{equation}
 \vartheta_{n,d}^{-r},\qquad
 \vartheta_{n,d}:=\binom nd(d/n)^d(1-d/n)^{n-d},
 \label{eq:fixed-row-conditioning}
\end{equation}
for $r$ selected rows.  Dense weights admit a separate Fourier bound.  The
needed fact is that a hypergeometric intersection count can be represented as
a sum of independent Bernoulli variables~\cite{HuiPark2014}; Parseval's
identity and Cauchy--Schwarz then compare the expanded word with a uniform
word.  This separation avoids paying the conditioning cost through the dense
range.

\subsection{Certified fixed-row examples}

The positive recurrence, the conditioned comparison, and the dense Fourier
bound give the following rate-$1/5$ certificates.  Here $k=2^s$, $n=5k$, and
$L=n/20$.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-row binary certificates]
\label{thm:fixed-row-certificates}
For each EA row of Table~\ref{tab:fixed-row-certificates}, independently
sample every expander row as a uniform weight-$d$ vector and apply the fixed
accumulator.  For each EC row, sample the same expander and independently
sample a wrapped convolution of memory $21$.  The corresponding probability
of $\Bad_L$ is at most the displayed value $U$.
\end{theorem}

EA & $30$ & $39$ & $2.796467641\cdot10^{-7}$ & $21.769891$\\
EC, $m=21$ & $20$ & $15$ & $5.160307473\cdot10^{-7}$ & $20.886039$\\
EC, $m=21$ & $25$ & $17$ & $4.259825504\cdot10^{-7}$ & $21.162702$\\

\bottomrule

\begin{proof}
For EA, the verifier evaluates the nonnegative recurrence
\eqref{eq:fixed-row-recurrence} at small supports.  It applies the conditioning
factor \eqref{eq:fixed-row-conditioning} at intermediate supports and the
dense Fourier bound described above at large supports.  For EC, the verifier
composes the same regional shell calculation with the exact wrapped transfer
\eqref{eq:ec-exact-enumerator}.  A conditioned Bernoulli comparison and an
invertibility bound cover the remaining supports.  In every row, the checked
intervals partition $[1,k]$, and outward-rounded arithmetic gives the
displayed upper endpoint.  Lemma~\ref{lem:certified-support-partition} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate-soundness} complete the proof.
\end{proof}

These values show that fixing the total row weight already removes much of
the sparse-message loss.  Region stratification improves further by forcing
each row to spread across the entire word.
