\begin{abstract}
Expand--Accumulate (EA) and Expand--Convolute (EC) codes combine a sparse
random linear map with an invertible recursive map.  Cryptographic
applications require the sampled generator to have no low-weight nonzero
codeword.  Published proofs replace the recursive state process by
concentration bounds.  We instead compute its exact input--output weight
enumerator.

For the original binary ensembles, the new bounds recover the
Gilbert--Varshamov exponent for messages of linear weight and reduce the
expander density needed for sparse messages.  We then replace independent
incidence by region-stratified regular incidence.  At rate one half, a
two-sided regular EC ensemble with left/right degrees $10/5$ and memory $15$
reaches $99.993\%$ of the binary Gilbert--Varshamov distance.  The probability
of sampling a generator below this distance is less than $2^{-32.4990}$.

We extend the construction to finite fields using independent nonzero edge
labels and full-field convolution coefficients.  These coefficients mix
field components; scalar extension of a binary matrix does not.  Over
$\F_{2^{128}}$, degrees $24/12$ with memory $5$ reach $98.415\%$ of the
rate-one-half Gilbert--Varshamov distance with sampling failure below
$2^{-45.03}$.  Outward-rounded verifiers certify every finite claim.  The
results concern minimum distance of sampled ensembles; they do not provide a
decoder or a complete protocol-security proof.
\end{abstract}
