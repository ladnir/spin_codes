\section{End-to-End Integrations}
\label{sec:integrations}

This section starts the end-to-end synthesis stage of the paper.
The goal is to show how the abstract framework of Section~\ref{sec:sc-framework}
is actually consumed by concrete outer/inner pairs.
We begin with the dense outer + dense inner pairing because it is the cleanest full
validation of the framework, even though it is not the eventual linear-time flagship.

\subsection{Validation pair: dense outer + dense inner}
\label{sec:integration-dense-dense}

Take as outer code the dense log-memory construction of Section~\ref{sec:outer-dense-logmem},
with memory
\[
M=\lceil c_{\mathrm{out}}\log_2 n\rceil,
\]
and as inner code the dense time-varying recursive construction of Section~\ref{sec:inner-dense},
with memory
\[
m=\lceil \gamma \log_2 n\rceil.
\]
The resulting serial concatenation has one random interleaver and \(O(\log n)\) taps per symbol
in both the outer and inner encoders, hence \(O(n\log n)\) total encoding work.

\begin{theorem}[Dense outer + dense inner]
\label{thm:dense-dense-integration}
Fix \(\delta\in(0,1/2)\) and choose \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\).
Assume the dense-inner tail bound of Section~\ref{sec:inner-dense} holds at \((\delta,\varepsilon)\), and let
\[
\rho:=1-\varepsilon.
\]
Let \(\alpha>0\), \(c\ge 0\), and \(\beta>1\) be the constants supplied by the dense-outer interface
(Section~\ref{sec:outer-dense-logmem}), and let \(a\ge 0\) be the constant supplied by the dense-inner interface
(Corollary~\ref{cor:dense-inner-implies-interface}).

If
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dense-dense-compatibility}
\beta\rho<1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\alpha\log\Big(\frac{1}{\beta\rho}\Big) > a+c,
\end{equation}
then the dense outer + dense inner serial concatenation \(\Csc=\Ctwo\circ \Pi \circ \Cone\) satisfies
\[
\Pr\bigl[d_{\min}(\Csc)\le \delta n\bigr]=o(1).
\]
In particular, \(d_{\min}(\Csc)=\Omega(n)\) with high probability over the outer randomness,
the interleaver, and the inner randomness.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:outer-dense-logdistance} and Lemma~\ref{lem:outer-dense-spectrum},
the dense outer satisfies Assumption~\ref{ass:outer-interface} for some constants
\(\alpha>0\), \(c\ge 0\), and \(\beta>1\).
By Corollary~\ref{cor:dense-inner-implies-interface}, the dense inner satisfies
Assumption~\ref{ass:inner-interface} with parameters \((a,\rho)\), where \(\rho=1-\varepsilon\).
Therefore Corollary~\ref{cor:linear-dist-from-interfaces} applies directly, and the conclusion follows
under the compatibility condition \eqref{eq:dense-dense-compatibility}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What this theorem is for.}
The dense+dense pairing is not meant to be the final flagship construction.
Its role is to validate that the framework can carry a complete minimum-distance proof from explicit outer and inner
ingredients all the way to an end-to-end linear-distance theorem.
It also isolates the key quantitative bottleneck:
for any proposed target distance \(\delta\), we must certify constants on the outer side
\((\alpha,c,\beta)\) and on the inner side \((a,\rho)\) that make
\(\alpha\log(1/(\beta\rho))\) dominate the polynomial loss.

\subsection{Dense+dense optimization target}
\label{sec:integration-dense-dense-piecewise}

To approach practically meaningful distances, especially at rate \(1/2\), the single-envelope theorem above is too coarse.
The dense inner already provides two different bounds that are useful on different weight ranges
(Corollary~\ref{cor:dense-inner-piecewise}), so the next step is to combine those with comparably sharp outer spectrum
envelopes via the piecewise framework corollary.

\begin{corollary}[Dense+dense piecewise reduction]
\label{cor:dense-dense-piecewise-reduction}
Fix \(\delta\in(0,1/2)\), choose \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\), \(\xi>0\), and a threshold \(W_\star\in[n]\).
Let
\[
L:=\lceil(2+\xi)\delta n\rceil,
\qquad
q_{\delta,\varepsilon}(n)
:=
\PP\!\left[\ \Binomial(\lfloor(1-\varepsilon)n\rfloor,\tfrac12)\le \delta n\ \right],
\]
\[
b_{\delta,\xi}(n)
:=
\PP\!\left[\ \Binomial(L,\tfrac12)\le \delta n\ \right].
\]
Suppose the dense outer satisfies an outer cutoff
\[
\Pr[d_{\min}(\Cone)<w_0]\le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{out}}(n),
\]
and admits expected-spectrum envelopes
\[
\EE[\enum_{\Cone,w}] \le A_w^{\mathrm{sm}}
\qquad (w_0\le w\le W_\star),
\]
\[
\EE[\enum_{\Cone,w}] \le A_w^{\mathrm{lg}}
\qquad (W_\star < w\le n).
\]
Then the dense outer + dense inner serial concatenation satisfies
\begin{align}
\Pr[d_{\min}(\Csc)\le \delta n]
\;&\le\;
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{out}}(n)
\;+\;
\sum_{w=w_0}^{W_\star}
A_w^{\mathrm{sm}}
\left(
\frac{w(w-1)}{n-1} + (L2^{-m})^w + w\,b_{\delta,\xi}(n)
\right) \notag\\
&\qquad\qquad
+\sum_{w=W_\star+1}^{n}
A_w^{\mathrm{lg}}
\left(
(1-\varepsilon)^w + n2^{-m} + q_{\delta,\varepsilon}(n)
\right).
\label{eq:dense-dense-piecewise-objective}
\end{align}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Corollary~\ref{cor:framework-piecewise} with the two weight ranges
\[
I_1:=\{w_0,\dots,W_\star\},
\qquad
I_2:=\{W_\star+1,\dots,n\},
\]
the prescribed outer envelopes on those two ranges,
and the dense-inner envelopes from Corollary~\ref{cor:dense-inner-piecewise}:
use \eqref{eq:dense-inner-smallw-envelope} on \(I_1\) and
\eqref{eq:dense-inner-global-envelope} on \(I_2\).
This yields \eqref{eq:dense-dense-piecewise-objective}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{This is the optimization problem we now care about.}
For a given blocklength \(n\), target distance \(\delta\), and inner memory \(m\),
the dense+dense proof problem is to make the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:dense-dense-piecewise-objective} small by choosing:
\begin{itemize}
\item the outer memory / tap parameter (which controls the outer envelopes \(A_w^{\mathrm{sm}},A_w^{\mathrm{lg}}\)),
\item the split point \(W_\star\),
\item the dense-inner parameters \(\varepsilon\) and \(\xi\).
\end{itemize}
This formulation is the bridge from the current asymptotic proof to concrete finite-length questions such as
\(n=2^{16},2^{20},\dots,2^{30}\) and to benchmark targets such as the binary Gilbert--Varshamov distance at rate \(1/2\).

\begin{corollary}[Dense+dense run-aware reduction]
\label{cor:dense-dense-run-aware}
Fix \(\delta\in(0,1/2)\), choose \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\) and \(\xi>0\), and define
\[
L:=\lceil(2+\xi)\delta n\rceil,
\]
\[
q_{\delta,\varepsilon}(n)
:=
\PP\!\left[\ \Binomial(\lfloor(1-\varepsilon)n\rfloor,\tfrac12)\le \delta n\ \right],
\]
\[
b_{\delta,\xi}(n)
:=
\PP\!\left[\ \Binomial(L,\tfrac12)\le \delta n\ \right].
\]
For \(1\le r\le w\), define
\[
\mathrm{RunTail}_n(w,r)
\;:=\;
\frac{1}{\binom{n}{w}}
\sum_{s=1}^{r-1}
\binom{w-1}{s-1}\binom{n-w+1}{s},
\]
and define the dense-inner optimization profile
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dense-inner-Bw}
B_w^{\mathrm{dense}}(\delta;\varepsilon,\xi,m)
\;:=\;
\min\!\left\{
(1-\varepsilon)^w + n2^{-m} + q_{\delta,\varepsilon}(n),\ 
\min_{1\le r\le w}
\Bigl(
\mathrm{RunTail}_n(w,r) + (L2^{-m})^r + r\,b_{\delta,\xi}(n)
\Bigr)
\right\}.
\end{equation}
Suppose the outer code satisfies an outer cutoff
\[
\Pr[d_{\min}(\Cone)<w_0]\le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{out}}(n),
\]
and admits expected-spectrum envelopes
\[
\EE[\enum_{\Cone,w}] \le A_w
\qquad (w_0\le w\le n).
\]
Then the dense outer + dense inner serial concatenation satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dense-dense-run-aware-objective}
\Pr[d_{\min}(\Csc)\le \delta n]
\;\le\;
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{out}}(n)
\;+\;
\sum_{w=w_0}^{n}
A_w\,B_w^{\mathrm{dense}}(\delta;\varepsilon,\xi,m).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Corollary~\ref{cor:framework-envelope} with the outer envelopes \(A_w\) and the inner envelopes
\(B_w^{\mathrm{dense}}(\delta;\varepsilon,\xi,m)\).
The validity of the latter follows from Corollary~\ref{cor:dense-inner-piecewise}, specifically from
\eqref{eq:dense-inner-best-envelope}, together with the exact run-tail formula \eqref{eq:run-lower-tail-exact}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why this dominates the earlier split-threshold reduction.}
The piecewise bound \eqref{eq:dense-dense-piecewise-objective} is still useful for quick asymptotic arguments,
but \eqref{eq:dense-dense-run-aware-objective} is the tighter quantity for actual optimization:
it no longer hard-codes a single threshold \(W_\star\),
and it lets the inner proof choose the best run target \(r\) separately at every weight \(w\).
This is exactly what we need once the outer spectrum is exponential on linear-weight scales.

\paragraph{Dense outer specialization.}
For the dense outer, Corollary~\ref{cor:outer-dense-spectrum-piecewise} supplies the exact expected spectrum
\[
\EE[\enum_{\Cone,w}] = A_w^{\mathrm{dense}}
:=
\sum_{\ell=1}^{k} N_\ell\,
\binom{r(\ell+M)}{w}\,2^{-r(\ell+M)},
\]
with \(N_1=k\) and \(N_\ell=(k-\ell+1)2^{\ell-2}\) for \(\ell\ge 2\).
Therefore the dense+dense optimization target becomes
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dense-dense-explicit-objective}
\Pr[d_{\min}(\Csc)\le \delta n]
\;\le\;
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{out}}(n)
\;+\;
\sum_{w=w_0}^{n}
A_w^{\mathrm{dense}}\,B_w^{\mathrm{dense}}(\delta;\varepsilon,\xi,m).
\end{equation}
The remaining task is to understand this bound numerically and asymptotically at rate \(1/2\),
and then to decide what memory sizes \(M\) and \(m\) are required to push the certified \(\delta\)
toward the Gilbert--Varshamov benchmark.

\paragraph{Rate-\(\tfrac12\) exponent picture.}
The new inner and outer profiles now give a concrete asymptotic optimization problem.
For linear weights \(w=\omega n\), Proposition~\ref{prop:outer-dense-rate-half-exponent} shows that the dense outer
contributes roughly
\[
A_w^{\mathrm{dense}}
\;\lesssim\;
2^{n\Phi_{1/2}(\omega)},
\]
while Corollary~\ref{cor:run-lower-tail-alpha} shows that choosing \(r=\lfloor \alpha w\rfloor\) contributes the
run-deficit exponent
\[
\Psi_{\mathrm{run}}^{(w)}(\omega,\alpha)
=
H_2(\omega)
- \omega H_2(\alpha)
- (1-\omega)H_2\!\Bigl(\frac{\alpha\omega}{1-\omega}\Bigr)
\]
for every \(0<\alpha<1-\omega\).
Thus, in the linear-weight regime, the dense+dense problem reduces to making the inner exponent dominate
\(\Phi_{1/2}(\omega)\) uniformly over the relevant \(\omega\)-range.

\paragraph{Why this is the right next benchmark.}
Near the rate-\(\tfrac12\) GV target, \(\omega\approx 0.11\), the dense outer lies on its linear branch
\(\Phi_{1/2}(\omega)=\eta_2\omega\) with \(\eta_2\approx 1.2716\).
So if the dense inner cannot beat that growth profile after optimizing over \(\alpha\), \(\varepsilon\), \(\xi\), and the
state size \(m\), then the sparse flagship constructions will be under real pressure as well.
This is exactly why the dense+dense pairing is the correct first quantitative stress test.
