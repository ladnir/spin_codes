\section{End-to-End Integrations}
\label{sec:integrations}

This section starts the end-to-end synthesis stage of the paper.
The goal is to show how the abstract framework of Section~\ref{sec:sc-framework}
is actually consumed by concrete outer/inner pairs.
We begin with the dense outer + dense inner pairing because it is the cleanest full
validation of the framework, even though it is not the eventual linear-time flagship.

\subsection{Validation pair: dense outer + dense inner}
\label{sec:integration-dense-dense}

Take as outer code the dense log-memory construction of Section~\ref{sec:outer-dense-logmem},
with memory
\[
M=\lceil c_{\mathrm{out}}\log_2 n\rceil,
\]
and as inner code the dense time-varying recursive construction of Section~\ref{sec:inner-dense},
with memory
\[
m=\lceil \gamma \log_2 n\rceil.
\]
The resulting serial concatenation has one random interleaver and \(O(\log n)\) taps per symbol
in both the outer and inner encoders, hence \(O(n\log n)\) total encoding work.

\begin{theorem}[Dense outer + dense inner]
\label{thm:dense-dense-integration}
Fix \(\delta\in(0,1/2)\) and choose \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\).
Assume the dense-inner tail bound of Section~\ref{sec:inner-dense} holds at \((\delta,\varepsilon)\), and let
\[
\rho:=1-\varepsilon.
\]
Let \(\alpha>0\), \(c\ge 0\), and \(\beta>1\) be the constants supplied by the dense-outer interface
(Section~\ref{sec:outer-dense-logmem}), and let \(a\ge 0\) be the constant supplied by the dense-inner interface
(Corollary~\ref{cor:dense-inner-implies-interface}).

If
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dense-dense-compatibility}
\beta\rho<1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\alpha\log\Big(\frac{1}{\beta\rho}\Big) > a+c,
\end{equation}
then the dense outer + dense inner serial concatenation \(\Csc=\Ctwo\circ \Pi \circ \Cone\) satisfies
\[
\Pr\bigl[d_{\min}(\Csc)\le \delta n\bigr]=o(1).
\]
In particular, \(d_{\min}(\Csc)=\Omega(n)\) with high probability over the outer randomness,
the interleaver, and the inner randomness.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:outer-dense-logdistance} and Lemma~\ref{lem:outer-dense-spectrum},
the dense outer satisfies Assumption~\ref{ass:outer-interface} for some constants
\(\alpha>0\), \(c\ge 0\), and \(\beta>1\).
By Corollary~\ref{cor:dense-inner-implies-interface}, the dense inner satisfies
Assumption~\ref{ass:inner-interface} with parameters \((a,\rho)\), where \(\rho=1-\varepsilon\).
Therefore Corollary~\ref{cor:linear-dist-from-interfaces} applies directly, and the conclusion follows
under the compatibility condition \eqref{eq:dense-dense-compatibility}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What this theorem is for.}
The dense+dense pairing is not meant to be the final flagship construction.
Its role is to validate that the framework can carry a complete minimum-distance proof from explicit outer and inner
ingredients all the way to an end-to-end linear-distance theorem.
It also isolates the key quantitative bottleneck:
for any proposed target distance \(\delta\), we must certify constants on the outer side
\((\alpha,c,\beta)\) and on the inner side \((a,\rho)\) that make
\(\alpha\log(1/(\beta\rho))\) dominate the polynomial loss.

\paragraph{Next synthesis step.}
The next integration target is to sharpen this theorem using the piecewise framework corollary
(Corollary~\ref{cor:framework-piecewise}) rather than the single-envelope specialization.
That will let us use the strongest dense-inner bound in each weight range and will serve as the template for the
eventual sparse outer + sparse inner flagship construction.
